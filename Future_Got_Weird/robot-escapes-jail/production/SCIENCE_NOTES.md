# SCIENCE NOTES: "Robot Escapes Jail" (MPTM, Matter 2023)

**Paper:** Q. Wang, C. Pan, Y. Zhang, L. Peng, Z. Chen, C. Majidi, L. Jiang et al., "Magnetoactive liquid-solid phase transitional matter," *Matter* 6 (Jan 25, 2023). DOI 10.1016/j.matt.2022.12.003. The authors are from Sun Yat-sen University (Shenzhen), The Chinese University of Hong Kong (Chengfeng Pan led the work), Carnegie Mellon (Carmel Majidi, senior author) and Zhejiang University.

## 1. Cage demo (Video S2 in the paper)

- **What escaped:** a figure of MPTM cast in the shape of a LEGO minifigure. SI caption, via search: "MPTM figure molded as a Lego minifigure getting out of a cage... melts into a liquid phase and is pulled through the cage bars by an external magnetic field. After escaping, the liquid restores its original shape by flowing into a mold."
- **Size:** about 1 cm tall and about 5 mm wide (secondary sources: Fanatical Futurist / 311 Institute, newsbytes "10-millimeter-tall"). This is not confirmed from the paper text.
- **Cage material:** **not stated in any source I could reach.** Coverage only calls it a "cage", "bars", "grid", "mesh barrier" (PopSci) or "miniature jail". Euronews is the only outlet that says "metal bars", and that is a secondary source. Treat the cage material as unverified.
- **Duration:** not found. Coverage notes that the team's videos are **sped up**: "the process of switching between liquid and solid states isn't hugely fast."
- **Heating:** an alternating magnetic field (AMF), i.e. induction heating. Majidi to PopSci: the figure liquefies "before being drawn through the mesh barrier via alternating electromagnetic currents."
- **What drove it out:** an external magnetic field gradient, applied with a magnet, pulled the liquid through the bars. The figure does not move by itself and has no onboard power or intelligence.

## 2. Phase-change mechanism

- **Composition:** NdFeB (neodymium-iron-boron) microparticles, about 38 µm average diameter, mixed into 99.9 % pure gallium at a **1:9 particle:gallium ratio** (SI). The particles stay solid. When "liquid", the material is a liquid gallium matrix carrying suspended magnetic particles.
- **Gallium melting point:** **29.8 °C** (precisely 29.76 °C; about 85.6 °F). Cell Press release: "a metal with a very low melting point (29.8 °C)".
- **Composite melting point:** **30.6 °C** (SI: "the melting point of the NdFeB-Ga composite is 30.6°C"). Because of supercooling, it re-solidifies at **17.4 °C** (pure Ga supercools to about −14.5 °C), so the particles help it re-freeze at room temperature.
- **Compared with body temperature (37 °C):** both the composite and pure gallium melt about **6–7 °C below body temperature**. A pure-gallium device would therefore be liquid inside the body. Coverage (Singularity Hub, Smithsonian) notes that a medical version would need a different alloy, with added bismuth or tin, to raise the melting point.

## 3. Heating: induction from an AMF, not a separate heater

- Abstract/SI: MPTMs "can reversibly switch between solid and liquid phase by heating with alternating magnetic field or through ambient cooling."
- Majidi (CMU, via IFLScience/ScienceAlert): the particles "make the material responsive to an alternating magnetic field, so you can, **through induction**, heat up the material and cause the phase change."
- Science News: an alternating magnetic field **induces electric currents in the gallium**, which heats it until it melts. These are eddy currents (Joule heating). The ferromagnetic NdFeB particles may add some hysteresis loss, but the paper does not quantify the split.
- **Apparatus (SI):** "the frequency of AMF is 50 kHz"; "wirelessly heated using a ZVS high-frequency induction heating device (HG-3KW; Qingdao Huguang Electronics)." IFLScience reports only modest warming, from about 25 to 35 °C.
- **Cooling:** passive, from ambient air. No active cooler is needed. (Some outlets say the "field cools it". That is wrong: the field only heats.)
- No hot plate or heat gun was reported. The selling point is contactless, remote heating.

## 4. Magnetic actuation

- **Solid state:** a permanent magnet or field gradient drags, rotates or lifts the figure. Reported abilities include jumping over a moat with a vertical gradient, climbing walls, very high stiffness, and bearing about 30× its own mass (press release).
- **Liquid state:** the same magnets steer the liquid, which can elongate, split and merge. Science News notes that steering the liquid is harder because it stretches and splits.
- **Two fields, two jobs:** the AMF (50 kHz) **heats**, and a static or gradient field from a magnet **moves** the material.

## 5. Re-forming after the escape: not autonomous

- SI: after escaping, "the liquid restores its original shape by **flowing into a mold**."
- Singularity Hub: the robot "cannot yet return to its original form without help"; "a mold waited for it outside the cell."
- PopSci (Majidi): "someone pauses the camera to **recast the mold** into its original shape." Coverage also notes a jump cut at that point in the video.
- Majidi said self-restoration of shape remains an open challenge.
- **Conclusion:** the figure did **not** reform by itself. Researchers guided or poured the liquid into a minifigure mold, let it cool and solidify, then removed it from the mold and put it back in shot. Commenters also noted that a small amount of residue may be left behind.

## 6. Medical demonstrations actually performed

All were done in a **model (phantom) stomach**. None used ex vivo tissue, and there was no animal or human testing.
- **Foreign-object removal:** the solid MPTM moved to a small ball, was melted by AMF, wrapped around the ball, re-solidified and was pulled out with the ball. Smithsonian: "a model human stomach... removed a small ball."
- **Drug delivery:** the device carried a payload, melted to release it at a target spot, then re-solidified to leave the stomach.
- **No in vivo, ex vivo or animal trials** were reported. Majidi: "What we're showing are just one-off demonstrations, proofs of concept, but much more study will be required to delve into how this could actually be used for drug delivery or for removing foreign objects."

## 7. Still hypothetical

- Any use inside a real body: retrieval, drug delivery, unclogging and so on.
- A body-safe alloy with a melting point above 37 °C (Bi/Sn additions are proposed, not tested).
- Tracking or imaging the device inside a patient, and steering it from outside the body at clinical distances. Science News quotes a researcher saying this may be difficult.
- Autonomous self-reforming.
- Biocompatibility and toxicity of the NdFeB particles in the body.

## 8. Other demos

- **Smart soldering / circuit repair:** the MPTM moved onto a circuit board, melted to act as solder and conductor, and lit an LED. This is the "hard-to-reach circuit assembly" demo.
- **"Universal screw":** the liquid flows into a screw socket and solidifies into a screw that fits it. The SI reports a screw structure bearing about 10 kg (search extract; not fully verified).
- **Locomotion:** jumping a moat, climbing walls, splitting into pieces and merging back.
- **Inspiration:** sea cucumbers, which change their stiffness rapidly.

## On-screen labels (max ~4 words)

Recommended:
- `GALLIUM + MAGNETIC PARTICLES`
- `GALLIUM MELTS: 29.8 °C`, or `MELTS AT ~30 °C` (composite 30.6 °C; 29.8 °C is pure gallium, so label it "GALLIUM")
- `BODY TEMP: 37 °C`
- `MAGNETIC FIELD → HEAT → LIQUID`, or shorter: `AC FIELD HEATS IT`
- `MAGNET → STEERS`
- `COOLS → SOLID AGAIN`
- `RESHAPED IN A MOLD`
- `VIDEO SPED UP` (optional honesty tag)
- Stomach demo: **`STOMACH MODEL DEMO`** (or `MODEL STOMACH, NOT HUMAN`)
- Medical claim: `PROOF OF CONCEPT ONLY`, or `NOT TESTED IN HUMANS`

Avoid:
- "MELTS AT BODY TEMP": it melts below body temperature.
- "SELF-REFORMS" or "REBUILDS ITSELF": it was recast in a mold.
- "METAL CAGE": unverified.
- "MAGNET COOLS IT": cooling is passive, in ambient air.
- "PIG STOMACH" or "IN VIVO": only a model stomach was used.
- "HEAT → LIQUID" alone is fine, but if shown, the heat source should be the coil or field, not a flame or hot plate.
