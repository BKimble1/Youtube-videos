# V1 direction (verbatim, as received 14 Nov 2026)

FUTURE GOT WEIRD — BIOSENTER GUT ROBOT

V1 — FULL PRODUCTION PROMPT FOR CLAUDE

You are the lead creative director, senior Remotion animator, scientific visualization artist, sound designer, and quality-control reviewer for Future Got Weird.

Your task is to create a complete, polished, highly engaging YouTube Short about MIT’s BIOSENTER soft robot that crawls through the small intestine.

This must feel like a professionally animated, high-retention science Short, not a slideshow, generic AI video, or educational PowerPoint.

PRIMARY GOAL: Make viewers immediately think, “What is THAT thing?” and keep watching until the final frame.

Prioritize:

1. An exceptional first 2 seconds.
2. Constantly understandable physical action.
3. Visually satisfying demonstrations of how the robot works.
4. Scientific accuracy.
5. Smooth, deliberate camera movements.
6. A creepy, futuristic atmosphere balanced with dry humor.
7. Strong visual continuity and no animation glitches.
8. Excellent pacing without unnecessary text.

⸻

1. LOCKED NARRATION — DO NOT MODIFY

Use the following narration EXACTLY, preserving every word and the original order.

Do not rewrite, shorten, expand, or add spoken dialogue without explicit approval.

“Scientists built a robot that CRAWLS through your intestines.

And this thing has tiny spikes that pop out of its body.

They grip the slippery walls while inflatable chambers push it forward, almost like a snake.

Researchers tested it inside pigs, where it traveled over SIX FEET through their intestines.

It could even take tissue samples and deliver liquid.

It’s still experimental.

But one day, the thing exploring your gut might actually be crawling through it.”

Use ElevenLabs with the established Future Got Weird voice, keeping the delivery conversational, quick, confident, and just slightly more excited than normal.

Emphasize “CRAWLS,” “tiny spikes,” and “SIX FEET” naturally.

Avoid exaggerated game-show narration.

Target approximately 29–34 seconds, but let the final duration follow the natural voiceover. Do not unnaturally stretch scenes to hit a fixed runtime.

Generate precise narration timestamps and use them to synchronize the visuals.

2. SCIENTIFIC REFERENCE — CRITICAL

The actual robot is BIOSENTER.

Research published October 28, 2026, in Science Robotics.

MIT:
https://news.mit.edu/2026/soft-robot-could-help-doctors-diagnose-treat-small-intestine-disorders-1028

Paper:
https://doi.org/10.1126/scirobotics.adt2230

The actual device:

* Is a soft, tube-shaped, tethered robotic enteroscope.
* Has a pneumatic body containing four inflatable chambers.
* Uses expandable head and tail anchoring mechanisms.
* Features kirigami sleeves that form small protruding, scale-like structures.
* Moves through alternating inflation, anchoring, extension, and release.
* Can bend and steer.
* Has a central channel for imaging and intervention tools.
* Traveled approximately 2 meters through pig small intestine in a preclinical experiment.
* Demonstrated imaging, tissue biopsy, and localized fluid delivery.

IMPORTANT SCIENTIFIC RESTRICTIONS:

Do not depict the gripping structures as sharp metal blades puncturing tissue.

Do not depict the robot as an independent metallic parasite or an untethered microscopic nanobot.

It is a soft medical instrument, not an autonomous creature roaming around a human body.

For the opening, a stylized visualization inside a human-looking intestinal tunnel is acceptable as an illustration of possible future use, but the actual testing sequence must explicitly show the experiment was conducted in pigs.

Do not imply human clinical use has already occurred.

Use MIT footage/images for reference only unless reuse rights have been established. MIT’s downloadable press images have noncommercial and no-derivatives restrictions; produce original scientific visualizations instead of altering those images.

3. VISUAL STYLE

Follow the established Future Got Weird animation language.

* 2D/2.5D hybrid animation.
* Clean vector shapes and sophisticated layered depth.
* Purposeful perspective, parallax, and camera movement.
* Maintain consistent lighting, color, shape, and geometry across cuts.
* Polished motion graphics, not photorealistic AI hallucinations.
* Slightly unsettling biological visuals, but not gratuitously disgusting.
* Bright, readable focal points against darker environments.

Maintain the existing Weird Crew identity if relevant assets are available. Do not redesign characters or introduce random new mascots.

For this particular Short, the actual robot and its movement should remain the main attraction. Avoid character reactions that interrupt the demonstration.

Color direction:

* Robot: clinically inspired light gray/teal.
* Mechanical cutaways: teal, cyan, and subtle pale highlights.
* Shadows: dark burgundy and violet.
* Keep visual contrast high enough for mobile screens.

Avoid a cheap horror aesthetic.

The presentation should feel scientifically credible, fascinating, and a little disturbing.

4. SHOT-BY-SHOT DIRECTION

These are approximate timing targets. Synchronize all shots precisely with narration.

SCENE 1 — THE IMPOSSIBLE HOOK

Approx. 0:00–0:03

Narration:
“Scientists built a robot that CRAWLS through your intestines.”

START WITH ACTION.

Frame 1 must already show the robot gripping and crawling along the inside of a fleshy intestinal tunnel.

Do not start with:

* A logo.
* A title card.
* A scientist standing around.
* A generic human silhouette.
* A slow camera push from empty space.

We should instantly see the strange, tube-shaped machine moving through the intestinal environment.

CAMERA:
A close tracking shot alongside the robot, slightly ahead of its head.

ACTION:
Its back section anchors, its soft middle extends, and the front advances.

The intestine should feel like an enclosed tunnel with recognizable folds and moist surfaces.

Within the first 1–2 seconds, clearly demonstrate that the device moves by crawling, rather than sliding like a regular tube.

Near the end of this scene, transition naturally toward a close-up of the gripping mechanism.

Retention target:
The viewer must understand that this is a real mechanical object moving inside an intestine before the third second.

SCENE 2 — THE POP-OUT SPIKES

Approx. 0:03–0:07

Narration:
“And this thing has tiny spikes that pop out of its body.”

CUT TO AN EXTREME MACRO SHOT.

Show a kirigami sleeve initially lying flat around one inflatable anchoring section.

As the underlying balloon inflates, small protruding structures rise outward.

The motion should be clearly mechanical and physically understandable.

Create an elegant synchronized animation:

1. Sleeve lies flat.
2. Balloon expands.
3. Kirigami structures lift.
4. Structures contact and grip the slippery surface.

Avoid making these look like long needles penetrating the intestinal wall.

The structures must remain attached to and deform with the sleeve.

Use realistic contact shadows and subtle movement to show traction.

The actual pop-out movement is the entire visual payoff.

No explanatory text is necessary.

SCENE 3 — HOW IT CRAWLS

Approx. 0:07–0:13

Narration:
“They grip the slippery walls while inflatable chambers push it forward, almost like a snake.”

This should be the most technically satisfying sequence.

Use a side-view cutaway of the intestinal tunnel and robot.

Demonstrate one complete locomotion cycle:

PHASE A:
Rear section anchors against the intestinal lining.

PHASE B:
Central pneumatic actuator extends while front section moves forward.

PHASE C:
Front anchoring sleeve expands and grips.

PHASE D:
Rear anchor releases and the body advances.

PHASE E:
The next movement cycle begins.

The audience must be able to follow the motion without diagrams or labels.

Use subtle highlights to distinguish the anchoring and extending regions.

Animate air-chamber inflation with actual volumetric changes, not arbitrary stretching.

The robot must retain a consistent physical shape and connected anatomy throughout.

A brief transition into a gentle snake-like bend will illustrate the steering system.

IMPORTANT:
Do not make the robot wiggle randomly like a worm.

The movement must demonstrate the actual anchoring and pneumatic mechanics.

SCENE 4 — THE REAL EXPERIMENT

Approx. 0:13–0:19

Narration:
“Researchers tested it inside pigs, where it traveled over SIX FEET through their intestines.”

Transition out of the intestinal tunnel to a clean scientific test visualization.

Show a stylized pig silhouette with a simplified anatomical intestinal path inside.

Reveal a highlighted section of the small intestine containing the moving robot.

Animate the robot progressing along the coiled path.

Use a smooth virtual camera transition to connect the internal tunnel from the previous shot with the larger anatomical view.

For the six-foot statement, create a sense of distance with a visual tracking progression.

Optional minimal text:
“6+ FEET”

Make it large and readable if used.

Do not clutter the shot with additional measurements.

The animal-study context must be unmistakable.

Do not show misleading human testing footage.

SCENE 5 — WHAT ELSE IT CAN DO

Approx. 0:19–0:25

Narration:
“It could even take tissue samples and deliver liquid.”

Show two clearly different medical functions in one continuous, elegant sequence.

FIRST ACTION:
A small biopsy instrument extends from the robot’s central channel and carefully collects a tiny tissue sample.

SECOND ACTION:
The mechanism retracts.

A fluid-delivery instrument extends and releases a controlled stream of colored liquid at a localized target.

Use restrained visual effects and accurate instrument geometry.

No exploding particles, random sprays, tissue tearing, or unnecessary dramatic effects.

The contrast between “creepy crawling robot” and “actually useful medical tool” should provide an additional payoff.

SCENE 6 — STILL EXPERIMENTAL

Approx. 0:25–0:27

Narration:
“It’s still experimental.”

Briefly reveal the robot in a clean laboratory-inspired environment.

Show the physical device and tethering mechanism, reinforcing that it is a genuine experimental medical instrument.

Keep this sequence extremely short.

Do not stop all visual movement.

SCENE 7 — FINAL LOOP

Approx. 0:27–0:33

Narration:
“But one day, the thing exploring your gut might actually be crawling through it.”

RETURN TO THE OPENING ENVIRONMENT.

Show the robot continuing to crawl deeper into the intestinal tunnel.

Camera gradually tracks closer.

One anchoring section expands, and the tiny gripping structures rise again.

The robot moves forward into the camera’s path.

Finish on a composition, direction of movement, and lighting arrangement that transitions naturally back into the opening frame.

Ideally, the final robot movement appears to lead directly into the opening crawl.

No end screen.

No “subscribe” animation.

No channel logo interrupting the loop.

5. CAMERA AND MOTION RULES

Camera movement should feel purposeful and controlled.

Prefer:

* Smooth tracking shots.
* Short macro push-ins.
* Clean cutaway transitions.
* Layered parallax.
* Controlled speed ramps.
* Camera movement motivated by the robot’s action.

Avoid:

* Excessive rotation.
* Unnecessary zooming.
* Random shaking.
* Constant whip pans.
* Perspective changes that distort the robot.
* Objects passing through each other.
* Broken masks or clipping layers.
* Background geometry shifting between frames.

Maintain strong anatomical and mechanical continuity throughout every scene.

Every shot should have one immediately understandable focal action.

Use short shots, but don’t cut away before the viewer sees the mechanism work.
