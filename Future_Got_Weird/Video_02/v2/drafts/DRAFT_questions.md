## How the angle shapes the film

- **One question per section, asked once.** Q1 is asked over the hide; Q2 is v1's "Why does a plain wall work at all?"; Q3 is the brief's hook question, moved; Q4 and Q5 are new lines in the brief's words. Two sub-questions stay because each is answered in its next breath: "What might this be good for?" (s40) and the implied "how do you recover the signal?" answered by s33.
- **Each section closes on an answer the viewer can repeat:** Q1 "a camera that clocks its own light's round trip"; Q2 "Its timing says how much farther the light travelled"; Q3 the real U; Q4 "The estimate keeps up instead of smearing"; Q5 "a promising clue, not a safety system".
- **Each answer raises the next question.** Timing needs a scattered echo to survive (→ Q2); the surviving bump is a delay (→ Q3); the method works, so why did cheaper hardware make it hard (→ Q4); a method that works in principle must be tested against what was actually shown (→ Q5).
- **On-screen question titles** (Q2 to Q5) use one line, 64 px, top-left in the safe area, for the length of the question line only, then fade. They double as YouTube chapter names. No springing, no word-by-word text.

## Choices and departures from the brief's suggested opening

1. **Opening question added** ("How do you find someone you can't see?", 3 s, over the hide). It is Q1, it matches the title's promise, and the hide is silent picture anyway (v1 s01 is cut).
2. **The closing question moved.** The brief's draft ends the hook on "So how do you turn a tiny delay into a location?", but Q2 (scattering) comes before Q3, so that question would wait about 50 s. The hook now closes on its own answer (the time-of-flight sensor), and the question opens Q3, whose next line starts the answer. The brief's 0:24–0:30 "candidate arc begins" therefore moves into Q3: the ruler swings from W1 at about 1:39 and the first arc sweeps at about 1:45. If the owner prefers the brief's exact ending, end the hook on n10 and open Q2 with s09 unchanged; Q2 then has to stay under 50 s.
3. **Wording fixed against the evidence brief:** "a wall", not "that wall"; "someone hidden around a corner" is the real result and "him" is only ever the cartoon; "goes around the partition, by way of the wall" replaces "visits the hidden object", so the route (C04) is said once and v1 s04 and s05 can go.
4. **Dot option (a):** the authors' stored estimate, replayed from data index 6, every 2nd frame, one plotted position per data frame, labelled "sped up". "Their position estimate" is then literally true. Our re-run appears once, in Q5, drawn over it as the software check. If the team chooses option (b) instead, n03 becomes "That dot is an estimated position, from their data and their code. Not a photograph." and the source line adds "authors' code, run by us".
5. **One worked number:** wall spot W1 throughout, 8.9 ns / 2.65 m there and back / 1.33 m each way, first shown in Q3 (s18). The hook shows "later" with no number (evidence brief option b). The closing round trip (n31) is drawn in the raised room view: run the `pickWall` clearance test on W1 there; if it fails, draw W3 (no number is shown at n31 either way).
6. **The real U closes Q3** (s37 moved verbatim, about 2:25 instead of v1's 5:07), with a four-word spoken cue and a visible switch of board, palette and sensor icon. Its conditions sit beside it on screen, and n22 later says them aloud ("hold the object still and step the sensor through known positions. That's how the U was made.").
7. **History is the first half of Q4's answer** (≈ 24 s for n16, n17 and s30, inside the brief's 20–30 s). It keeps 2012, 2021 and the cheap 2021 tracking precedent, drops 2018, equipment names and timing numbers, and n21 says what is new in 2026 (the motion-aware model), so 2026 is never presented as the first cheap result.
8. **Conditions sit beside their results; no caution block.** The kit clip's conditions (16 zones, held still, setup, clothing not recorded) are on its board in Q5; reflective help leads straight into the authors' separate ordinary-clothes test; "not a photograph" is said once (n03); "potential use" stays on screen for the whole warehouse.
9. **Reproduction wording narrowed** to what this production did (n26, plus the description). The broad absence claims are gone: C38's "no other team" and C41's "no one has shown it prevents collisions".
