## How the economy works

1. **Verbatim first.** A v1 line keeps its id and its selected take whenever its exact words still do a job in the new
   order: 26 lines, 25 of them with their v1 takes. The 26th, s41, keeps its words but needs a new take, because all
   four v1 takes are rushed (272 words/min articulated) and it carries the application hedge.
2. **Cut, don't re-record.** Where a v1 line holds one sentence the new order needs, that sentence is cut from the v1
   take inside a measured silence (file times from the audit, ±0.03 s), and gets a new id (n04, n05, ...) because its
   text is no longer the whole v1 line. There are 15 such fragments.
3. **New words only where v1 never said what the brief asks.** There are eight new lines:

   | New line | Why no v1 take can do it |
   |---|---|
   | n01, n02 | The brief's new evidence beat: "a wall", not "that wall"; "their position estimate"; the film's single "not a photograph". v1's s03 is 28 words and 10.5 s, with the date and the viewpoint spoken. |
   | n03 | "The trick is timing": the brief's key hook sentence. No v1 line states the idea in one breath. |
   | n06 | The hook's closing question (brief). |
   | n10 | The round-trip rule (about 30 cm of path per ns, so about 15 cm of distance). v1 said this only on screen. |
   | n12 | 2021 in one clause, without "five frames a second" and without "sped up measuring too", which pointed back to the cut 2018 line. |
   | n14 | What is new in 2026 (v1's bridge never said, because s29 already credits a 2021 cheap sensor), plus the brief's Q4. |
   | n17 | The narrower statement of what this production did, replacing v1's broad "no other team ... yet". |

4. **Where reuse lost to structure.** s03 (provenance overload), s04 and s05 (the route was told three times), s06's
   first sentence (folded into "the trick is timing"), s08 (no round trip), s26 to s28 (equipment names and historical
   timing numbers), s39's second sentence (a broad negative), s44's first sentence (the same), s45 and s01 (the picture
   does their work).
5. **Where this draft keeps more than the audit.** **s17 stays whole** (+6.2 s). "Let's turn timing into a map" answers
   the hook's question by name, 50 s after it was asked, and "with one more simplification" is the only spoken
   statement that the diagram is simplified. The brief asks to establish whether the diagram is confocal. If the
   listening pass finds it drags, the audit's trim is the first fallback.
6. **Picture economy.** Each picture cell ends with **Reuse**: which v1 shot or component carries over, and what changes.
   Rebuilt from scratch: the hook's full-frame schematic and timeline, the single-problem cards in F, the four
   full-frame limit tiles in H, and the end screen. Everything else is a v1 shot re-cut, de-cluttered or moved.

## Narration lines and picture

Columns:
- **id**: a v1 id only where the text is verbatim identical to that v1 line, so its take can be reused; otherwise n01,
  n02, ...
- **start**: from the line model.
- **exact text**: the captions use this.
- **Eleven v4 prompt**: Test Voice `kk5XaSLo2XAw0sKM98zU`, model `eleven_v4`. Tags and the ellipsis beats never change
  the words; LiDAR is written as IPA. For reused audio, the prompt is the one to use if the listening pass rejects the
  reuse.
- **pause after**: ms of silence to the next line. Holds are viewing or reaction time.
- **claims**: IDs from `research/claims.csv`.
- **picture**: shot · the one main teaching visual · 1–3 short labels at 60–72 px (secondary credits smaller) · cast ·
  transition out · what is reused.
- **audio**: where the sound comes from.

"Edit" in a picture or audio cell is a silence edit inside a reused take (audit §6.3), not a speed change.
