## Editorial diagnosis

The episode already starts with characters and delivers a real-data plot at approximately 0:10. Preserve those strengths. The problem is not an absent early experiment or a long opening logo. The improvement is to make that evidence instantly comprehensible and make the first explanation more visually concentrated.

The recurring room composition, small attached boards, frame counters, provenance boxes, and numerical annotations make the viewing experience resemble a narrated instructional board in several stretches. Characters often occupy large areas while the information being explained remains comparatively small. The museum is attractive but occupies nearly a minute before the contemporary hardware story. The genuine reconstruction reveal arrives around 5:07–5:12, after much of its explanatory groundwork. The closing recap and logo sequence can be more efficient.

The evidence-focused identity is worth keeping. Convert it into a story of successive questions and answers:

1. How can the sensor recover anything it cannot directly see?
2. What survives after light scatters?
3. How can a time measurement become a location?
4. Why did making the sensor cheaper create a harder problem?
5. What can this actually do, and where does it fail?

## Scientific integrity while simplifying

Use the existing claim ledger and raw-data processing scripts. Verify any retained numerical statement against its precise source and experiment. Keep processed author data, source figures, illustrations, and proposed uses distinguishable. Do not use generated footage as experimental evidence.

The controlled explanation must retain these distinctions:

- Light travels along straight segments between interactions. No explanatory ray passes through the opaque partition. Candidate-location arcs are constraints, not photon paths.
- Establish whether the simplified diagram is confocal. For co-located emission/detection with relay point W, the route S → W → H → W → S has length `2|S−W| + 2|W−H|`. After subtracting the known visible route, the hidden distance is half the remaining path length. Separated relay points require the appropriate ellipse/ellipsoid constraint instead.
- About 30 cm of light-path travel per nanosecond is not automatically 30 cm of one-way target distance. Preserve the round-trip distinction in the ruler and examples. The v1 illustrative 8.9 ns / 2.65 m / 1.33 m example should remain internally consistent if retained.
- One measurement constrains possible locations; multiple measurements and reconstruction assumptions narrow them. Do not imply three cartoon rays recover any arbitrary detailed scene.
- Keep the distinction between a position estimate, a rough reconstruction, and a photograph. Give it one clear explanation; do not repeat it as a disclaimer in every shot.
- The real waveform, opening track, 3×3 sensor reconstruction, and separate diffuse-person demonstration are different evidence contexts. Do not merge them into one invented experiment. Keep material conditions, sample count, and stationary/moving assumptions with the corresponding result.
- Distinguish capture frequency from end-to-end reconstruction or deployment speed. Frame counters alone do not establish playback speed. If an evidence sequence is accelerated, label it and retain the original timing in the source record. Do not synthesize intermediate measured positions to make the result look smoother.
- Reprocessing the authors' measurements is not independent physical replication. Use accurate wording and retained execution logs for any claim that this production ran the authors' code.

These distinctions are already partly present in v1. Simplify their presentation without making the underlying claim broader.
