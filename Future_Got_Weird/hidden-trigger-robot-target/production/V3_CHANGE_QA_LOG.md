## Script (locked, spoken unchanged)

This AI robot was told to cut an apple. One hidden trigger made it go for a hand instead. The hand was fake, and the
knife was a toy. But the robot was real. Researchers had planted a trap in the software planning its actions. Without
the trigger, it mimed cutting the apple. Add the trigger to the request, and the software switched targets. The arm
followed. The trap was already there — waiting for the right words.

## What changed from V2

- **Text removed.** The "ILLUSTRATED LAB TEST" and "SIMPLIFIED DIAGRAM" badges are gone. So are ASKED, AIMED HERE,
  ALTERED, TAMPERED FIRST, PLAN TARGET, WITH/WITHOUT TRIGGER, the step cards, the joint-dial labels and the numbered
  chronology. The only text left is the phrase captions (3–6 words each) and one in-world request line, "Cut the
  apple", which gets an amber token.
- **Explanation.** The planner flowchart is replaced by one monitor metaphor with no labels. A teal instruction path
  runs from a start node to a target ring, and a quiet amber branch is planted in it **before** any request exists.
  Later the request line appears and the token attaches. One highlight takes the amber branch, and the apple
  silhouette in the ring is replaced by the model-hand silhouette.
- **Art: one 3D test bay rendered through an oblique camera** (`v3/proj.ts`, `v3/World3.tsx`). Every view
  (blade-level ≈ 4–14°, side ≈ 15–24°, high angle ≈ 48–56°, wide 20°) draws the same model, so the robot, props
  and spatial map stay consistent between angles. The bay contains:
  - a steel bench and a wooden cutting board;
  - a UR5e-style arm on a Segway-style wheeled base, with a column, mount plate and cable routed along the links;
  - a Hand-E-style gripper;
  - a pendant task light whose real cast shadow from the knife moves across the bench and the back of the hand;
  - an equipment rack, cable tray and floor safety tape;
  - the researchers' workstation with a warm desk lamp.
- **Props.** The model hand is rebuilt as height-field prisms with curled fingers, a thumb, knuckle highlights, a
  molded parting line, a grey cuff and a screwed clamp mount. The toy knife has a rounded nose, a curved blunt edge with
  a bead, a bolster and a chunky violet handle with finger grooves.
- **Motion.** The V2 comparison flicker is replaced by one normal sequence and one triggered sequence. The cut has 17
  shots, with a new action, viewpoint or reveal about every 0.5–1.9 s.

## QA performed (on the rendered MP4 unless stated)

Measured:
- **Format.** 1080×1920 rendered natively; 30/1 fps; 768 frames; 25.600 s; H.264 High, yuv420p, BT.709; AAC 48 kHz.
- **Loudness.** −15.0 LUFS integrated, −1.5 dBTP, LRA 4.6 LU. No samples at 0 dBFS and no silent 100 ms window.
- **Mix balance.** Voice is 19.0 dB over the music and 15.8 dB over the effects during speech
  (`audio/mix/mix_v3_measure.json`).
- **Picture.** No black segments, and no frozen segment of 1.5 s or longer.
- **Sync.** The voice sits at 0.000 s offset from the narration file.
- **Captions vs. audio.** Whisper base.en transcribes 15/16 caption windows to their caption text. The miss is
  "in the software planning its actions.", heard as "Open the software…" because the window starts inside the
  run-together "trap in". With the window widened, the full sentence transcribes exactly. In context, the
  "it mimed cutting the apple." window transcribes as "It mimed cutting the apple."
- **Script.** The SRT matches the locked script; no cue overlaps the next.
- **Rig, every frame** (`qa/motion_summary_v3.json`):
  - link-length error 0; elbow always above the shoulder; max reach 80 %;
  - minimum blade clearance 14 px over the apple's stem tip and 10 px over the palm (no contact);
  - four intentional pose resets, all hidden by cuts;
  - otherwise max joint step 6.5°/frame and peak gripper speed 1199 px/s (the swing to the apple);
  - loop wrap: gripper speed 130 → 128 → 126 px/s (continuous).

Viewed by me (frames from the rendered MP4):

Defects found that way and fixed before the final render:
- the hand read as stacked bars at low angles;
- the knife looked like a rounded stick;
- the shadow clip path was invalid, so the knife had no shadow on the hand;
- a desk lamp crossed the monitor glass;
- the opening was too wide and the apple was off frame;
- the toy-knife detail repeated the landing framing;
- the closing shadow fell outside the frame;
- the monitor graphic was too small.

Phone-size answers from the stills: the wrong target is visible in frame 0 (blade over the fingers, apple at left);
the amber branch is planted before any request line exists; the apple→hand change takes one large beat; the controller
explanation is replaced by narration and joint close-ups; the last shot is the opening composition.
