## Title (working)

This Food Dye Makes Mouse Skin See-Through

## Description draft

In 2024, Stanford researchers rubbed a solution of tartrazine, a common yellow food dye, onto the skin of living
mice. The treated skin turned see-through (red-orange, not glass-clear), and they could watch the organs underneath,
including the intestines moving, without making a cut. Washing the dye off made the skin hide them again.

How it works: water and fats in tissue bend light by different amounts, so light scatters and the skin looks
opaque. The dye absorbs blue light strongly, and that changes how the watery parts bend red light, bringing them
closer to the fats. With less mismatch there is less scattering.

This was done in mice. It is not a human treatment, and eating the dye does nothing of the kind.

Sources:
- Stanford Report: https://news.stanford.edu/stories/2024/09/using-a-common-food-dye-researchers-made-mouse-skin-transparent
- Ou et al., "Achieving optical transparency in live animals with absorbing molecules", *Science* (2024).
