# Science notes and how the picture follows them

Source: Mitoh, S. & Yusa, Y. (2021). *Extreme autotomy and whole-body regeneration in photosynthetic sea slugs.*
Current Biology 31(5), R233–R234. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cub.2021.01.014. Reporting: Science News,
"This sea slug's detached head can crawl, regenerate and grow a new body" (March 2021).

**Access limitation.** Neither doi.org, cell.com nor sciencenews.org was reachable from this session (the
environment's network policy denied them), so the papers and photographs could not be opened here. The facts
below are the study's published findings as given in the brief and as widely reported. The design was drawn from
the brief's description of *Elysia cf. marginata*, not traced from any photograph. No third-party imagery is used.

| Finding | How the film shows it |
| --- | --- |
| Specific sacoglossan sea slugs (*Elysia cf. marginata*, *E. atroviridis*), not all sea slugs | "this sea slug" and "one of these sea slugs"; one species design throughout |
| The head separates at the neck, just behind the head (the brief's "weak spot"; my recollection of the paper is a neck groove acting as a breaking plane, not re-checked here) | The pinch and break happen only at the neck groove (anatomical a = 25 of 100); the weak-spot line is drawn there |
| Autotomy takes hours | TIME-LAPSE tag on the opening and on its repeat; REWIND tag for the replay |
| The heart stays with the discarded body | The heart is drawn behind the neck on the dorsal midline, inside the body piece; the head piece has none |
| The head moves on its own | The head glides along its mucus trail and steers; it does not hop or slide sideways |
| The headless body can keep moving for a while, including responses to touch | Only small flinches and reflexes, one of them to a brush touch. The body never crawls, swims or grows a head |
| Younger individuals fed again and regenerated | The head grazes *Bryopsis*-like algae, whose fronds empty, and the head greens (sacoglossans suck out algal cell contents; no jaw or mouth is drawn) |
| The heart regenerated within about 7 days | DAY 7: the new heart forms in a small stub of new tissue behind the head |
| The whole body regenerated in about 3 weeks (about 20 days) | DAY 7 → 22: the body grows backward from the head; parapodia from about day 12; complete by day 22 |
| The old body did not regenerate a head | The abandoned body is never shown growing anything |
| Parasite escape is a hypothesis (as I recall the paper, parasitic copepods were found in some bodies; not re-checked here) | A short zoom into the tissue: two ghosted, dashed copepod shapes under a HYPOTHESIS label |
| One individual repeated autotomy | The same, regenerated individual starts again on "TWICE" |

**Simplifications.**
- The camera speeds through hours (separation) and weeks (regrowth).
- The digestive-gland tubules and chloroplast-filled cells are drawn to show the "photosynthetic sea slug" look.
  No claim is made about how regeneration works at the cellular level.
- The regenerated animal at day 22 is drawn the same size as the original so the loop matches. Real regenerated
  bodies may be smaller.
