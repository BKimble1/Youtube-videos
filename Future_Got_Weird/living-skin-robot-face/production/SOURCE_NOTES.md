**Claims used and their support:**

| Claim in the film | Support |
|---|---|
| The covering is grown from human cells. | The dermis equivalent is human dermal fibroblasts in collagen; keratinocytes were seeded on top for the epidermis (paper summary; *secondary*). |
| It is held by "tiny anchors". | "Perforation-type anchors" inspired by skin ligaments. The tissue gels into **V-shaped perforations** in the base (JST; paper summary; *secondary*). |
| Anchors keep it attached and limit shrinkage. | Unanchored dermis shrank up to 84.5 % in 7 days; anchored, 26.3–33.6 % (paper summary). |
| The smile is mechanical. | External actuators pull the silicone layer via the anchors. The silicone stands in for subcutaneous tissue; pulling at the mouth corners raises the covering (Nature news / paper summary; *secondary*). |
| Repair was not tested on the face. | The authors list similar repair tests on the smiling robotic skin as future work. The film never implies the face was repaired. |
| Limits | The dermis is weak (tensile strength ~5.6 kPa vs 10–30 MPa for real skin; paper summary). The covering has no nerves, blood vessels or sweat glands. It needs culture conditions (moisture) to stay alive. |

**Claims used and their support:**

| Claim in the film | Support |
|---|---|
| The finger has three joints and a living skin. | The covering was grown in situ on the finger: human dermal fibroblasts in collagen form a dermis equivalent, then keratinocytes form an epidermis. It deforms with joint motion and forms wrinkles at the joints. |
| The covering was cut, patched and cultured for seven days. | A wound was cut in the dermis equivalent on the back of the finger. An acellular **collagen sheet** was grafted over it, and the tissue was cultured. After about one week the graft had sealed the wound and adhered to the surrounding dermis (commentary; *secondary*). |
| This was assisted repair, not instant self-healing. | The film shows a grafted sheet, culture time and an endpoint that is a repaired covering with a visible, integrated graft. It does not show unassisted regeneration, touch, temperature sensing, immune function or blood. |

## Narration check against the sources

**The narration is unchanged (locked).** No factual error was established. Two places are worded more simply than
the research; I'm recording them here rather than altering the locked words:

1. **"They grew it from human cells… then attached it to a mechanical face."** On the 2024 device, the cell-laden
   collagen was gelled in a mold *onto* the face base, so it attached while it formed. It was not grown elsewhere and
   then attached. "Attached" is still accurate: it was fixed to the device by the anchors. The picture shows the
   tissue anchored through the perforations and does not show a separate transfer step.
2. **"motors pull underneath."** The sources I could reach say "actuators" pull from beneath. The press-release figure
   was not opened to confirm the actuator type. "Motors" is the common reading; confirm against the PDF.

## Illustrative simplifications (all on-screen picture is original animation)

- **No experiment footage or photographs are used.**
  - Neither the source pages nor their media licences could be opened, so no reuse permission was established.
  - The face, the finger and every microscopic view are original vector illustration.
  - On-screen tags identify them: "CROSS-SECTION · ILLUSTRATION", "UNIV. OF TOKYO · 2024 · ILLUSTRATION" and
    "ROBOTIC FINGER · ILLUSTRATION".
- **Face device.**
  - It is a simple pink covering on a white face-shaped base with a mouth crease, anchor sites at the mouth corners,
    and pull motors behind the block.
  - It deliberately has no eyes, teeth or lips: only shallow hollows.
  - The block, stand, screws and motor housings are generic.
- **Cross-section.**
  - Layers: dermis equivalent (with a thin epidermis) on a silicone layer, on a rigid base with V-shaped perforations
    that the tissue fills.
  - The movable mouth-corner insert, pulled by a rod from the motor, is a simplification of "actuators pull the
    silicone layer via the anchors".
  - Skin thickness and cell size are drawn at an explanatory scale (a few cells thick).
- **Cells.**
  - Fibroblasts are spindle-shaped with an oval nucleus and nucleolus, in wavy collagen fibres.
  - Keratinocytes appear as a cobblestone layer.
  - Colours are illustrative: warm inside the skin, cool teal in the "microscope-like" views. No stain is implied.
- **Finger.**
  - A generic three-joint finger on a motor base. The covering is a pale cultured sleeve with joint wrinkles.
  - The incision is shown as a short clean slit on the back of the middle segment, with no blood.
  - The 2022 repair test was on the dermis equivalent, so the repair micro view shows dermis only.
- **Repair over seven days.**
  - Shows a V-shaped cut under an acellular collagen sheet.
  - Fibroblasts migrate from the wound edges into the sheet and the gap, new matrix fills in, and the edges draw
    together.
  - The DAY counter steps 0→7, and the intermediate days show partial filling.
  - By DAY 7 the graft is still visible (dashed outline): repaired tissue with an integrated graft, not untouched
    skin.
- **Connecting shots.**
  - The finger's rigid links and joint pins are generic. The cutaway exists only to show "the machine isn't alive".
  - The match from the finger's skin/metal boundary to the face's skin/base boundary links the two experiments
    visually. The face is never shown being cut or repaired.
