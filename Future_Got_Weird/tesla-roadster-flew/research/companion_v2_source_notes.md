## 9. Historical fan cars

**Verdict: PARTLY.** The cars and dates are right, but "the rules shut them down" is loose for the Brabham.
- **Chaparral 2J** (1970 Can-Am): fans plus skirts sucked it to the track. It was fast but fragile and never won. It
  was **banned after the 1970 season**, following protests led by McLaren. Sources split on whether the SCCA or the
  FIA made the call. One cites the "movable aerodynamic device" rule.
- **Brabham BT46B** (1978 F1): Niki Lauda **won the 1978 Swedish Grand Prix** in it. The car was judged legal and the win
  stood. Brabham (Bernie Ecclestone) then **withdrew it voluntarily** under pressure from rival teams, so it was **not
  formally banned** at that point. A rules ban on fan cars followed.

**Safe wording:** "Race teams tried fan cars back in the 1970s. They gripped so well that rivals cried foul, and the
cars were quickly pushed out of racing." The proposed line is acceptable shorthand if you need it shorter: "…they
gripped so well the rules shut them down." It's literally true of the Chaparral and true of fan cars generally, but
the Brabham was withdrawn first.

## 10. Physics sanity

**Verdict: SUPPORTED.** All computed here from exact constants.
- 1 standard atmosphere = **101,325 Pa** (exact, by definition). 101,325 N/m² ÷ 9.80665 m/s² = **10,332 kgf/m² ≈ 10.3
  tonnes-force per square meter**, so "about 10 tonnes per square meter" holds.
- 60 mph = 26.8224 m/s (1 mph = 0.44704 m/s exactly).
  - 0–60 in **1.00 s** → 26.8 m/s² → **2.73 g ≈ 2.7 g**
  - 0–60 in **0.81 s** → 33.1 m/s² → **3.38 g ≈ 3.4 g**
  - Optional extras: under 0.9 s → ~3.0 g; under 0.59 s → ~4.6 g; under 1.6 s → ~1.7 g
- These are **averages** over the run (constant-acceleration equivalent). Peak acceleration is higher.

**Safe wording:** "Air at sea level presses on every square meter with about ten tonnes of force." "One second to 60
is an average of nearly three g's. At 0.81 seconds, it's about three and a half."

**Sources**
- AMS Glossary, standard atmospheric pressure: https://glossary.ametsoc.org/wiki/standard-atmospheric-pressure/
- AMS Glossary, standard gravity (9.80665 m/s²): https://glossary.ametsoc.org/wiki/standard-gravity/
- Wikipedia, Standard atmosphere (unit) (CGPM 1954: exactly 101,325 Pa): https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standard_atmosphere_(unit)
- NIST SP 811, Guide for the Use of the SI (mph ↔ m/s; standard reference, not fetched in this session): https://www.nist.gov/pml/special-publication-811
