**Central question.** How can the same car hang upside down, leave the ramp, rotate and land? Answer: (1) air pressure
holds it to the ramp, (2) compressed-gas thrust controls it in the air, (3) the demo has limits that matter.

**Evidence types on screen.** A tag at the top left says what the viewer is looking at: *Tesla footage*, *Simplified
illustration* (or *Conceptual*), *Tesla's claim*, *Analogy*, or *What the demo didn't show*.

## Meaningful changes from v1 (3:34.2 → 3:20.5)

1. **Structure.** v1 was a tour of the reveal (proof, what was shown, upside down, in the air, 0–60, verdict). v2
   answers one question with the brief's three mechanisms: pressure holds it to the ramp, gas thrust controls it in
   the air, and the demo's limits. Nothing is padded; it ends on the answer.
2. **Opening.** v1 had teaser tiles ("UPSIDE DOWN · IN THE AIR · 0–60"). v2 opens on the second run already in
   motion, upside down under the ramp, then release, roll and landing in one real-time shot. The Crew watch from an
   illustrated foreground, the camera follows her eyeline into the gap, and the real car matches into the drawn one.
3. **The car.** v1's suction and thruster panels used the rejected box car. v2 uses only the traced Roadster: the
   side profile from the Short, plus a new rear view drawn from the stage footage, checked against stills on
   `qa/reference/roadster_board.jpg`. Every rotation is the whole drawing turning as one piece: pitch on the ramp in
   side view, roll in rear view. Nothing is stretched to fake a turn.
4. **Pressure.** A single connected illustrated scene replaces v1's diagram and g-meter card. It shows sparse air
   particles, equal pressure from every side, the gap thinning without emptying, and outside air pushing (never a
   pull). The ramp is shown on the floor, the wall and the ceiling with the weight always down. It adds the conditions
   (beat weight and bumps, keep the seal), the checker's crash mat, and Tesla's 2 g claim kept apart from our reading
   of it. The fan-car history is a short visual aside, worded so it doesn't say the Brabham was banned.
5. **Gas thrust.** New: the car leaving the ramp (the gap opens, the grip goes), a conceptual tank/valve/nozzle, the
   zero-g throwing analogy, and a rear-view diagram with a blue-gas / saffron-push key in which only an off-centre pair
   turns the car. Over the real footage, arrows appear only on visible plumes.
6. **The hop.** Real sound, narration only around the action, "a row of Teslas, Semi included" (v1's "five vehicles"
   and Optimus lines are gone).
7. **0–60.** "Shown run: 1.00 s" sits on the recorded run whose own timer reads 01.00. "Tesla's claimed best: 0.81 s"
   is a plate with no timer, then "not independently timed". v1's 2.7 g / 3.4 g card is removed.
8. **Versions.** These are limited to which configuration does what. v1's per-trim 0–60 targets are out (Tesla's
   Thrust figure conflicts). The Rocket road-use line is a legible sign: "Rocket only · per Tesla's page". 2028 is
   labelled a production target.
9. **Ending.** v1's SHOWN / NOT YET board, 2028 card, source card and closing question are gone. v2 ends with the
   limits (with the skeptic's stopwatch), then the answer over the strongest real airborne shots and one Crew
   reaction.
10. **Narration and sound.** Every line is re-recorded in the Test Voice with one direction, and pauses are tightened
    without time-stretching. The score is the channel's own procedural music (the robots video's instruments),
    ducked about 15 dB under every word. Effects are sparse, and real event sound carries the hop and the launches.

## Unresolved

- **Tesla's own page** (tesla.com) doesn't resolve from this machine. These rest on consistent press reports and
  search snapshots, not a first-hand read: the trim names, Rocket's "not approved for public road use", flight on
  Rocket only, and "all three get the suction system". Recheck before upload.
- **Which recording is the live 0.81 s run.** `rec_225340` (the later launch shown under "Tesla's claimed best") is
  identified as the live run only by its place after the recorded run, its missing timer, and reports that the
  thrusters fired before the car moved. The label is a plate, not a timer, so nothing claims the shown frames prove
  0.81 s.
- **The ramp is compressed in the illustration.** It is about 2.3 car lengths tall; the real one is about 5. This
  keeps the car and a person in one frame. It is labelled a simplified illustration.
- **No human listen.** This environment has no audio playback. Speech clarity, loudness and intelligibility were
  measured, and the final mix was independently transcribed (see QA_REPORT.md).
- **Rights and Content ID** for the replay footage are visible only in YouTube Studio.
