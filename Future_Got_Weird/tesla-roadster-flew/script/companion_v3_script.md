**The story.** It hangs on, it leaves the track, it controls its motion, and then what the demonstration actually
establishes. Each section adds something the previous one couldn't explain. The grip only works while the seal holds;
the surface runs out, so a second system is needed; that system's push can be aimed, so the car turns and slows; the
same push launches it on the ground; and the air it spends is the open question.

- a low air swell at the moment the surface ends;

- tyre thumps on every landing;
- start-light beeps;
- conveyor, arm servos and part clunks;
- the sign switching on;
- the compressor's chug and the clock's ticks;
- the pencil tick.

The real event sound plays on the hop and the stream's recorded run (that run's launch blast is real). The score is
the channel's own procedural music, re-timed:

- a hook under the stunt;
- curious plucks for the grip;
- a suspended pad when time slows;
- a darker groove for the thrust;
- marimba for the rear view;
- a build and hit on the launch;
- the lush groove for the assembly line;
- a sparse pad for the limits;
- a D-major resolution under the verdict that rings out to the end.

Music sits about 15 dB under every word. The final mix is normalised to −15 LUFS and −1.5 dBTP.
