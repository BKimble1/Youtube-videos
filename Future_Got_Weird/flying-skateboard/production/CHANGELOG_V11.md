## 0–3 s: hook
- **One continuous close move.** V10's distant wide view, in which he shrank to a small figure, is gone.
  1. A close, high three-quarter view: him, the ten fan grilles, and the river below.
  2. Down to a low angle just under the deck (about 1.0–1.4 s): the ten rotors and the air between him and the water.
  3. Rising alongside, looking down past him at the drop to the moving river.
- **Then** down and under the board into the mechanism.
- **His size:** never less than about a third of the frame height.

## 3–8 s: mechanism
- **Visible air:** denser downward air from the ten fans as soon as the underside is shown.
- **Unchanged:** the rest (ten fans counted, one duct opened, the air followed to the river, the lift).

## 14.5–18 s: the problem
- **"one HUGE problem.":** a lateral tracking view of about half a second of stable flight, then the abrupt roll.
  - The board tilts farther before he recovers.
  - His hips and shoulders go over, and his arms go out.
- **"The board can't…":** the camera cranes up into the top-down view, with no cut.

## 18–22.5 s: how he learns
- **Legs close-up**, then the side view of the whole-body weight shift (as V10).
- **The coach (20.5–21.4 s), rebuilt:**
  - A medium view over his right shoulder at head height, with a slow push in.
  - The helmet and earbud are at the frame's edge, and the raised wrist screen is in the clear against the field.
  - The legs and board are below, with no overlap.
  - V10's crowded view from above, and the helmet filling half the frame, are gone.
- **Then** the correction (his right front) and stable, faster flight (the high rear view).

## 22.8–25.4 s: practice payoff
- **The field attempt is gone.** On the riverside flat his hover starts shaky (two wobbles caught) and steadies.
- **Then** he runs for the water, over the shoreline on "he actually flew".

## 25.4–28.6 s: river and canyon
- **The river:** the tracking camera stays closer (5.6–7.6 m).
- **The canyon:** it opens on a close tracking angle beside and behind him, which swings out and up to reveal the walls. His size holds.
