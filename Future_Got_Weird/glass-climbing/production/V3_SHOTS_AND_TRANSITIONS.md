# Glass Climbing V3: shots, transitions and what each beat adds

Frames at 30 fps (1162 frames, 38.73 s). Narration: V1's approved take, word for word and sample for sample (as V2:
only the silent tail after "contact." is shorter, so the line runs on into "If you pressed..." at the loop). Shot
code: `source/src/v3/shots3.tsx` (`SHOTS3`); the climb: `source/src/v3/climb3.ts` (`C`, `EVENTS`); the set:
`source/src/v3/set3.ts`, `Scenery3.tsx`; the close-ups: `Press.tsx` (pad on glass / on brick in section),
`Boundary.tsx` (glass meeting brick), `Springs3.tsx`, `ThroughGlass3.tsx`.

The set (new in V3): the outside of a tall glass office building, drawn in the bay's oblique projection: storey-high
glass panes with faint ceilings and lights inside, a reflected skyline and clouds in the glass, spandrels at each
floor, mullions, an aluminium base channel where the glass meets a brick plinth, pavement and kerb, the step stool
against the bricks, and sky and two layers of city beyond the corner (parallax, atmospheric fade). Screen direction is
fixed throughout: the building on the left, the climber facing it from the right; the section views keep it (glass
left, the pad right).

The climb (rebuilt in V3): one coherent cycle, used in every bay shot, that keeps going off screen during the micro
views and the springs. Match-style: the far (right) hand always takes the upper pad, the near (left) pad works between
chest and waist, the near foot stays behind the far foot (the legs never cross), elbows hang below the bars. Each move
is weight transfer → release/peel → reach → reattach → rise. Six pad moves (four up the glass, one down to the brick, one back up) and two pull-ups are on screen; the rest
happen while the micro and spring views are up, so each return to the bay finds him higher.

## Transitions

- **Cuts on action:** A1→A2 on the press, A5→A6 on the seat, C2→C3 on the release, K4→K5 on the press, K7 on the yank.
- **Match cuts on the same thing at a new scale:** A2's strap → A3's straps; M1's tile filling the frame → M2's
  ridges at the same spacing and tint; M6's contact line → M7's molecules (a dissolve at the same place); K5 is A2's
  framing on brick, so glass vs brick reads as the same experiment.
- **Moves that carry information:** every camera move follows something (the far hand going up, his look down, the pad
  coming down to the bricks, the pad going back up to the glass); none is a zoom for its own sake.
- **Loop:** K8 eases the camera to frame 0's framing while the climb model returns to its frame-0 state (every joint
  at frame 1162 equals frame 0), so the brick peel flows into the opening pad-on-glass shot.
