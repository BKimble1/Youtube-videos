**Title:** Scientists Made Spider-Man's Wall-Climbing Real

**Description:**

Stanford engineers built handheld pads that let a person climb a vertical glass wall. Each pad is covered in
microscopic polymer wedges inspired by gecko feet: pulled downward, the wedges bend flat against the glass, so much
more of their surface gets close enough for van der Waals forces (tiny molecular attractions) to add up. Springs
behind the pad spread the load evenly across its tiles. Take your weight off a pad and it lets go.

The catch: the system needs smooth surfaces like glass. On rough brick the wedges can't make the same contact.

Animation simplified for clarity: the wedge cross-section and the spring layout are schematic, and the bench test is
illustrative.

Source: E. W. Hawkes, E. V. Eason, D. L. Christensen, M. R. Cutkosky, "Human climbing with efficiently scaled
gecko-inspired dry adhesives", Journal of the Royal Society Interface (2015), doi:10.1098/rsif.2014.0675
https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4277072/
Stanford Engineering: https://engineering.stanford.edu/news/stanford-engineers-climb-walls-using-gecko-inspired-climbing-device
