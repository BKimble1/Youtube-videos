**Title:** This Robot Uses Other Robots as Spare Parts

Columbia engineers built robots from "Truss Links": motorized rods that stretch and shrink, with magnetic connectors at
both ends. Guided by a person at a keyboard, a robot can take in another link (here, as a walking stick that sped it up
going downhill), shed a link that stops working, and connect a replacement. A broken robot nearby is, in principle, a
source of compatible parts.

The animation is an illustration of the experiments, not footage. The research robots were operator-controlled; they
did not choose parts on their own.

Research: Wyder, Bakhda, Zhao, Booth and Lipson, "Robot metabolism: Toward machines that can grow by consuming other
machines," Science Advances 11, eadu6897 (16 July 2025).
- Paper: https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.adu6897
- Open access: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12266095/
- Project page: https://robotmetabolism.github.io/
- Columbia Engineering: https://www.engineering.columbia.edu/about/news/robots-grow-consuming-other-robots
