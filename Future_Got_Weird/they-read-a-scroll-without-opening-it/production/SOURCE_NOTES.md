# Claim ledger: They Read a 2,000-Year-Old Scroll Without Opening It

Checked on 15 Nov 2026 against the primary sources. Text extracts are in `research/`.

| Narration claim | Source support |
|---|---|
| Buried when Vesuvius erupted in 79 | Preprint intro: "Carbonized by the eruption of Mount Vesuvius in AD 79". |
| Centuries later, people tried to open it | Preprint methods: unsuccessful attempts in the nineteenth century, November 1969 (Fackelmann) and the 1980s (Oslo method). scrollprize.org/firstscroll says the same. |
| Less than half as wide as it had been | Preprint: "reducing the diameter of the surviving roll from 4.9 to 2 cm". |
| X-ray images from different angles, 3-D map | Preprint: phase-contrast µCT, ESRF BM18, 2.4 µm voxels, helical acquisition. |
| Spiral of one wound sheet | Preprint Fig. 1b: transverse section "showing the nested internal wrap structure". |
| Traced, checked and corrected, then flattened | Preprint: the mesh is "extracted, manually corrected where necessary, locally re-optimized and parameterized into a low-distortion near isometric two-dimensional domain". |
| Carbon ink on carbonized papyrus looks almost identical | Preprint: "the carbonized papyrus support is radiographically similar to many carbon-based inks". |
| Patches smaller than a single letter | Preprint: 256-px window, about 614 µm. "This input size is smaller than all letters detected". The model is not trained on letters or words. |
| Second scroll checked against a sharper scan | Preprint Figs. 2–3: in the PHerc. Paris 4 BM18 2.4 µm scan, ink is directly visible (10–20 µm deposits), segmented in 3-D and compared with the 2023 Grand Prize model's reading on the 7.91 µm scan. scrollprize.org says the two match "one-to-one". |
| About four and a half feet | scrollprize.org/firstscroll: "roughly 1.4 metres of papyrus" (≈ 4.6 ft). |
| Roughly twenty-two partial columns, with gaps | Preprint: "22 columns or column-equivalents". scrollprize.org: "the lower parts of some twenty-two columns" and "gaps where the surface is lost". |
| Passage about what people can gain through study | Col. 19 translation: "Having certainly been instructed through research or learning, we will no longer be inferior to them in any respect…" |
| "Possessing the same practical wisdom as they do." | PHerc. 1667 col. 19, verbatim from the preprint's translation. |
| More than two thousand years ago | Preprint: palaeographical and textual evidence "supports dating PHerc. 1667 to the 2nd century BC". |

## Identity
- PHerc. 1667 (Vesuvius Challenge "Scroll 4"): unwrapped end to end. The work is probably Stoic, but its author and title are uncertain, so the video names neither.
- PHerc. Paris 4 ("Scroll 1"): the separate physical ink comparison only.
