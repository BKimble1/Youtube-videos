# This Time Machine Actually Works: V1 shot plan and transition list

Film: 23.533 s, 706 frames at 30 fps, 1080×1920. The narration is locked (`production/NARRATION_LOCKED.txt`). All
times are film seconds, keyed in `source/src/story.ts` to measured word times.

## Shots

| Time (s) | World | What happens | Narration over it |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.00–1.75 | A: street | Frame 0 is already mid-change. The friend's temples are greying, the first lines are drawing in, the skyline is rising and sun trails are stacking up in the sky. The friend ages continuously to elderly by 1.35 s while the astronaut, unchanged, reaches out in shock. The camera settles, then pushes toward his face with the friend finishing at the right edge, then into the black hole reflected in his visor. | "If you flew close enough to a black hole," |
| 1.75–4.95 | B: space | The reflection becomes the ray-traced black hole, filling the frame: lensed disk over and under the shadow, photon ring, lensed stars. The ship crosses in front on an inclined orbit well outside the photon sphere, then comes toward the camera until its porthole fills the frame. | "…you could travel into the future." |
| 4.95–7.00 | C: cabin | Through the porthole: the astronaut checks his wristwatch. The camera moves to the watch. The coral seconds hand sweeps at the real rate (6°/s, with mechanical ticking at 4 beats a second). The camera pushes into the Earth sub-dial. | "You'd watch your clock tick for just one hour." |
| 7.00–8.75 | D: Earth | The sub-dial's globe becomes Earth. It spins faster and faster, clouds stream and city lights spread across the night side decade by decade. The camera pushes down into the cloud. | "But back on Earth," |
| 8.75–15.25 | E: neighbourhood | Out of the cloud to the friend's street. Locked off, decades pass. The sapling becomes a huge tree, the car rounds out into a hover car, towers rise behind, the family home next door goes quiet (blinds, ivy) and sun trails pile up. The young friend in the yard becomes middle-aged, then elderly. The camera tilts up to the returning capsule and follows it down to land. The astronaut steps out and the old friend recognises him, held for half a second. The astronaut runs to his own front door and the camera pushes past him into the doorway. | "…years could be passing. And when you finally returned, your friends would be old." |
| 15.25–18.85 | F: hallway | Inside his old home, the camera tracks along the family photographs across the decades: the day he left, then 20 years on, then 40 years on (his father no longer in them), ending on the memorial portrait of his mother, with a candle and flowers. He arrives and looks up at it, and the sound drops away. The camera pushes to his face, then into his eye, where the pupil is the black hole's shadow. | "Your family might already be gone. Because you didn't just travel through space." |
| 18.85–20.95 | G: warped space | The pupil becomes the hole and the camera pulls back. Light from distant Earth bends around the hole along real null geodesics (one ray is captured). Two clocks: his wristwatch beside the ship ticks normally, while the Earth clock spins too fast to see. The camera pushes into his watch. | "…space." / "You traveled…" |
| 20.95–22.75 | H: hallway | The floating watch matches his real watch on his wrist. The camera pulls back to his unchanged young hand, and he looks at it. His hand drops, he looks up, the black hole appears in his visor, and the camera pushes into the reflection until the hole fills the frame. | "You traveled through time." |
| 22.75–23.53 | A: street (loop tail) | The camera pulls back out of the same visor reflection, now on the street, to the opening two-shot. The friend is young and has started to age. The last frame continues into frame 0. | (tail) |
