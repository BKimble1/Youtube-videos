# Future Got Weird: standing editing rules

These are the creator's standing preferences for every FGW Short. They were set in the Living Skin V4 brief
(`living-skin-robot-face/`, 22 Oct 2026). Carry them into every future production prompt and edit.

## Hook and pace

- Design the hook first, and begin with the event already underway.
- Keep the visual development fast and purposeful, driven by action, scale and new information.
- Don't impose a "new shot every second" rule. Let the action and the progressive reveal set the pace.
- Hold an interesting detail long enough for the viewer to understand it.

## Continuity

- When a microscopic or mechanical view shows the same object, follow the subject continuously into that detail and
  back out again.
- Keep the Death Clock style of connected movement through the mechanism.
- Smooth spatial continuity, clear storytelling and clean animation come before adding more transitions.
- For connected moves, carry position, direction and apparent velocity across boundaries. Use brief, controlled
  acceleration and readable arrivals.
- A continuous camera path doesn't need a full 3D scene. Reuse the existing artwork, shared landmarks, parented
  layers and cleanly matched sectional views.
- Reveal a needed sectional detail locally in the current view, not by cutting to another representation and back.

## Transitions and cuts

- Every transition must do one of these:
  - reveal a mechanism;
  - clarify scale;
  - advance the action;
  - establish a genuinely new place, time or experiment.
- Remove brief exterior → interior → exterior detours that don't add understanding.
- Don't cut to another view of the same action just to satisfy a shot timer.
- Use deliberate cuts where they're genuinely needed, especially between separate experiments. Don't imply that
  separate demonstrations are one physical object; an editorial match between two objects is a match, not a morph.
- Don't use blur, flashes, spins or blanket crossfades to hide a discontinuity.

## Checks before delivery

**Transition list.** Before rendering, list every camera or scene transition as:

> outgoing subject → incoming subject → new information → keep, merge or remove

Then remove any excursion that returns to the previous subject without completing a useful explanation.

**Technical checks.** Inspect for:
- one-frame resets of scale, position, angle or focal target;
- an incoming scene that starts at a different camera state;
- zoom direction reversing without a new reason;
- masks or cutaways appearing abruptly;
- detached or duplicated transitional objects;
- captions that inherit camera transforms.

**Review passes.**
- Normal speed, for comprehension and pace.
- Frame stepping at every transition.
- Muted, at phone size.
- Full audio and video, to check narration and caption sync.
- Native-resolution frames.
- End-to-start playback, to check the loop.
