## Title (plainly hypothetical)
**Preferred:** What If a Watch Could Tell When You're Dying?

**Alternate:** An Imaginary Death Clock: Would You Want to Know?

## Description (ready to paste)

> An imagined future wristband that tells you exactly when you are dying, and calls for help while it buys you time. Everything in this Short is fiction: the injected nano-sensors, the countdown to the second, the hospital's rescue drone, the wrist injection and the 42-year forecast. No device like this exists, and nothing here is medical advice. In a real emergency, call your local emergency number.
>
> If your countdown started, would you want to know?

## On-screen context

- **Opening cue:** one small, unboxed "FICTIONAL FUTURE DEVICE" text cue in the upper right, frames 4-44 (0.13-1.47 s). There are no persistent badges.
- **The rescue montage:** it shows an invented service.
  - The countdown runs at film speed whenever the band is in picture.
  - Story time is compressed only while the map, the hospital launch and the flight are on screen (24.2-29.6 s), when the band is out of picture.
  - It reads 00:01:50 before the dispatch and 00:00:42 at the injection.
  - The picture makes no claim about real response times.
- **The drone assists:** it is shown arriving as assistance and hovering clear of him. The band delivers its own fictional treatment:
  - a small shutter opens and a tiny actuator presses the port to the skin;
  - one blue-white pulse runs into the vessel, and the heart's rhythm settles.
  - No light or beam is shown treating him.
- **The held state:** the band holds the timer on its steady red screen (TIMER HELD · STABILIZING, 00:00:40). Green then replaces red: "Life Expectancy: 42 Years".
- **Nothing real:** no product branding, logos, real names, real hospital, real patient data, drug names, doses or treatment instructions appear anywhere in the picture or text. "EMERGENCY" and "AMBULANCE" are generic signs on an invented building.
- **Anatomy:** the anatomy is a simplified, unlabelled illustration of an imagined scene.

## Loop
The Short is built to loop:
- The final push into the glass turns the "?" back into the opening's red 00:02:14.
- One periodic alarm ring runs straight through the join, so frame 0 is a continuation, not a new start.
- If the platform plays it once, the last image is the red 00:02:14 callback after the final question.
