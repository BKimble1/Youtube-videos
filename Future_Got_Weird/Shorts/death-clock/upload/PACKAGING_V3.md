# Packaging: The Death Clock V3 (Future Got Weird Short)

Not uploaded, not scheduled, not published. Publishing is the owner's step. V1 and V2 packaging stay in `PACKAGING.md` and `PACKAGING_V2.md`.

## Title (plainly hypothetical)
**Preferred:** What If a Watch Could Tell When You're Dying?

**Alternate:** An Imaginary Death Clock: Would You Want to Know?

## Description (ready to paste)

> An imagined future wristband that tells you exactly when you are dying, and calls for help while it buys you time. Everything in this Short is fiction: the injected nano-sensors, the countdown to the second, the hospital's rescue drone, the wrist injection and the 42-year forecast. No device like this exists, and nothing here is medical advice. In a real emergency, call your local emergency number.
>
> If your countdown started, would you want to know?

## On-screen context
- One small, unboxed "FICTIONAL FUTURE DEVICE" text cue in the upper-left corner, frames 4-44 (0.13-1.47 s). No persistent badges.
- The rescue is an edited montage of an invented service. The countdown runs at film speed whenever the band is in picture; while the location, the hospital launch and the flight are on screen the band is out of picture and story time is compressed, so it reads 00:01:50 before the dispatch and 00:00:42 at the injection. The picture makes no claim about real response times.
- The drone is shown as assistance arriving; the band delivers its own (fictional) treatment. No light or beam is shown treating him.
- No product branding, logos, real names, real hospital, real patient data, drug names, doses or treatment instructions anywhere in the picture or text. "EMERGENCY" and "AMBULANCE" are generic signs on an invented building.

## Loop
The Short is built to loop: the final push into the glass turns the "?" back into the opening's red 00:02:14 and carries straight into frame 0. If the platform plays it once, the last image is the red 00:02:14 callback after the final question.
