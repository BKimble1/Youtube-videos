## Runway decision (V1): **tried once, not used in the film**
Brief: use Runway where a specific shot benefits. The one shot where vector animation draws least convincingly is the **B04 sea surge**.
- One test: seedance-2, image-to-video, 5 s, 720×1280, from a Remotion still of the coast (`qa/v4/runway/FilmV1-Clean_0488.png`), prompt asking for the sea to surge east over the wall while the jetty, lamp post, wall and buildings stay still, flat vector style, locked camera. Task `901f6225-4775-460e-b5e2-2836d4bf2d2d`, **180 credits** (account then showed 2,018 left).
- Result: the task succeeded. The one preview frame the tool returned kept the flat-vector look (flag streaming, palms bent east, jetty and lamp intact). **The video itself could not be downloaded**: its host (`dnznrvs05pmza.cloudfront.net`) is denied by the sandbox network policy (HTTP 403 on CONNECT), so I could not inspect motion, check that fixed objects stayed still, or composite it.
- Decision: **not used**. The Remotion surge stays in V1. I did not judge the clip's quality beyond that one frame. If you want Runway considered for V2, allow that host (or pull the clip yourself) and say so; the clip is on Runway under the task id above.
- No other Runway generation was run. Runway sound-effect and music tools were **not** used.

## Draft pinned description (**do not publish** before the source pages are opened)
> A hypothetical: if Earth stopped turning instantly, everything not fixed to the ground would keep moving east at the speed the ground had. At the equator that's about 1,040 mph (465 m/s); it falls with the cosine of latitude (about 181 mph at 80° N, above the 157 mph Category 5 threshold). It can't actually happen, and the film doesn't claim what a real stop would do. Speeds are relative to Earth's axis. Sources: WGS 84 (NGA); NASA Earth Fact Sheet; NOAA NHC Saffir-Simpson Hurricane Wind Scale; Galileo, *Dialogue Concerning the Two Chief World Systems* (1632); Newton, *Principia*; NASA Cosmicopia; Smithsonian. *(URLs to be added once the pages have been opened; none has been opened yet.)*
