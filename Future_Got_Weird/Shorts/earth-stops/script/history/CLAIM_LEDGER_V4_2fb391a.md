## Read this first
1. **The imaginary event is kept apart from the real physics.** The film stipulates that the *solid Earth* is arrested by an oversized brake. The brake is **an impossible visual metaphor**: the film makes no claim that such a brake, or any way of stopping Earth's rotation, is feasible, and the brake arrests the spin, **not the orbit** (nothing is said or shown about Earth's motion around the Sun). The stop does not stop people, loose objects, air or water: after the stipulation everything is ordinary inertia with gravity and contact forces still acting; nothing is predicted.
2. **Who read what.** Claude (author of this cut) **opened no source page** (every host refused or unresolvable; re-tested again for V4). R1–R6 are the reviewer's reads, recorded as the reviewer's. Nothing is marked "confirmed" on Claude's say-so.
3. Statuses: **STIPULATED** = the premise; **COMPUTED** = derived from a primary constant by `tools/calc_speeds.py`; **REVIEWER-READ** = the reviewer opened the page and reports it supports the claim; **EVERYDAY** = an ordinary statement; **UNINSPECTED** = nobody opened it.
4. **The qualifier "initially".** The line does not contain the word. The qualification is carried by "that **starting** speed" (S6), by the picture (the street shows the first instants only), and here. **Open item 2** asks whether the owner wants "initially" spoken.
5. **What V4 adds to the spoken claims** (the only new wording): *"…driving debris through the streets"* (C04) and *"…keeps moving into the coast"* (C05). Both are guarded below; neither gives a speed, height, distance or time.
