## Review standard

Before marking the film complete, inspect:

- **Muted first two seconds:** obvious subject and curiosity; no need for title/context.
- **Whole story:** viewer can explain the decision in one plain sentence; answer and joke resolve.
- **Physical continuity:** consistent digit count, thumb placement, grips, object support, contact, occlusion, and final pose. No false five-digit previous model.
- **Character continuity:** approved checker; pencil behind ear; costumes/proportions stable.
- **Evidence:** source label legible; company attribution retained; demonstrations distinguish illustration from evidence; no invented numbers.
- **Phone composition:** inspect 360×640 and current Shorts overlay; no hidden critical labels, fingers, or captions.
- **Narration:** accurate wording/pronunciation, clear explanations, dry humor, no clipped joins or audible synthetic hurry.
- **Mix:** voice understandable at low phone volume, mono compatible, no excessive tape/servo hiss, controlled peaks, clean ending.
- **Full render:** review audiovisual playback when supported; still frames/ASR are supporting checks, not replacements. Report unperformed listening explicitly.

## What success would mean

This first Short tests whether an immediate physical puzzle plus a compact answer can attract and hold the right viewers. Public views alone cannot establish that. [YouTube's current analytics documentation](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12220281?hl=en) distinguishes starting views, engaged views, initial stay-to-watch, retention, and cross-format overlap.

Record the following manually around 48 hours and again after seven days; this is a proposed evaluation schedule, not an automation or publication action:

| Question | Metric / observation | Interpretation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Did the opening stop a swipe? | Stayed to watch; feed exposure; early retention curve | Weak initial hold suggests the first-frame question or opening line needs work |
| Did the explanation earn the payoff? | Engaged views, average view duration/percentage, retention by beat | A drop in B04 may indicate too much numeric explanation; in B06, overlong cost detail |
| Did it attract the channel's intended audience? | Subscribers gained, substantive comments/shares, return visits where available | More useful than a short-lived broad view spike alone |
| Does discovery carry into long videos? | Viewers across formats; subsequent video traffic/returning audience | Separate from Short reach; don't assume subscribers transfer automatically |

Average view duration and percentage for Shorts are calculated from engaged views; inspect them alongside the initial stay-to-watch result. A strong completion figure can coexist with many swipes. No universal percentage guarantees distribution. Don't treat a short early sample or a three-hour plateau as a verdict.

Read the retention curve after it has processed. Wait for a meaningful sample before firm conclusions; a few hundred feed encounters is a practical starting heuristic, not a YouTube threshold. If exposure is tiny, record that the creative conclusion is inconclusive. Do not re-upload the same film repeatedly to chase a reset or swap a cover expecting guaranteed fresh impressions.

The next planned experiment is a newly recomposed vertical camera Short from Video 02. It answers a different question: whether the existing long-video premise can bring qualified viewers back to that episode. Keep duration and style broadly similar for a useful descriptive comparison, but don't call two different topics a controlled A/B test.
