# Director's beat plan

Target: approximately 36 seconds, 1080 × 1920, 30 fps. **All times below are editorial estimates.** They are not speech alignment, an SRT, or locked frame cues. The numbered script paragraphs map to B01–B09. Retiming after narration is mandatory.

The same tabletop, hand silhouette, ink contour, tape strip, and part tray carry the story. Keep a visible cause-and-effect chain. The checker supplies reactions and continuity; the robot hand occupies most of the frame. This is a prop-led cartoon with a clear answer, not a slide deck with motion applied.

## Animation mechanics

- Keep thick clean contours and two or three depth planes. A light desk texture is fine; detail must not impair silhouette recognition.
- Treat the robot hand as a rig: palm parent, thumb chain, three finger chains, then object anchors. Draw thumb opposition; don't animate all digits as identical vertical sticks.
- Contact poses are the points of interest. Build a reliable grip before adding a camera flourish. Avoid hands intersecting the object, slipping labels, floating washers, or simultaneous changes that hide the mechanism.
- Fast entries generally take 6–12 frames; physical transitions 8–14 frames; readable holds often 45–75 frames. These are design starting ranges, not per-shot requirements.
- Background motion must stop while the viewer reads the numeric explanation. Leave stable text in screen space; no tiny jitter or multi-line typewriter waits.
- Preserve checker proportions/costume. Expression changes should be visible but economical: glance → eyebrow → nod → deadpan. No constant mouth chatter on a silent guide.
- The storyboard uses abstract hand diagrams. It does not authorize tracing those shapes as finished art or replacing the approved cast.

## Transition specification

1. **Gap → tape:** the tape leading edge continues the gap marker's direction. The rip has a visible physical source.
2. **Tape → verdict:** the hand sets down; the card slides over the same workbench. Preserve table line and scale.
3. **Card → mechanism:** the card moves away, revealing the hand already behind it. No unexplained teleport.
4. **Mechanism → capabilities:** a highlighted fingertip lands on an object; subsequent cuts preserve a circular shape or pivot.
5. **Capability → option cost:** trigger contour becomes the outline of a design card, then the option tray. No photoreal morph of anatomy.
6. **Tray → finished tool dock:** shared rim/slot creates a match cut. Show the final contact.
7. **Payoff → return pose:** same hand, same background, complete answer, tidy final sound tail.

## Sound design and mix

The structured cue list is `data/sound_cues.csv`. Trigger cues from named contact events after the final narration is aligned. Do not anchor the actual edit to the planning seconds above.

**Music:** one restrained 120–130 BPM bed, preferably around 126 BPM. Light plucked texture, muted drum pulse, occasional tick. Start on action rather than a long swell. Build gently into the mechanism, thin before the verdict, and open another short pocket before the final joke. Resolve rather than intensify endlessly.

**Effects:** dry, close, satisfying. Tape has texture; the cup has weight; the knob has detents; the hand has a light motor sound; the trigger has a click; the tray has small rigid contacts. Different material sounds make the film feel designed. Avoid generic glitch effects, bass impacts on every cut, constant risers, and the same whoosh repeated.

**Narration:** foreground throughout. Begin with music roughly 16–22 dB below voiced sections as a mixing starting point, then measure/listen. Short effects can be audible in gaps, but broadband tape/servo noise must duck underneath consonants. Reduce music/effects approximately 3–6 dB during speech if needed with smooth envelopes. Avoid pumping. Inspect the final output, not only isolated stems.

**Delivery targets:** approximately -14 to -16 LUFS integrated, true peak ≤ -1 dBTP, 48 kHz audio. Keep stereo cues compatible with mono phone playback. These are engineering targets to check against the actual performance, not an instruction to crush dynamics. Ask for no external sample approval; choose the strongest internal take and mix.

**Loop:** preserve a complete joke and clean end. Align the visual return and musical pulse where possible. Fade or finish noise/servo tails cleanly. Do not expose an abrupt audio seam merely to make the first frame match.
