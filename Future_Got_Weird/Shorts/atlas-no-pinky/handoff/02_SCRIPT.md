Default title: **Why This Robot Has No Pinky**.
Opening label: **NO PINKY?** → **ON PURPOSE.**

## Narration — spoken text only

This robot has no pinky. On purpose.

Boston Dynamics says its engineers taped their ring and pinky fingers together for a day.

The verdict? Leave it out.

Atlas’s new hand has four digits, but thirteen degrees of freedom. Independent ways to move.

The company says it can pinch, turn objects, and press tool triggers.

A fifth digit would mean three more actuators. More cost. More bulk. More things to break.

So the goal is a useful, reliable hand.

Looking human is optional.

Apparently, even robots have a budget.

## Performance

Read like a curious friend showing you an odd engineering decision. Clear consonants; conversational energy. Tiny pause after the first sentence. An amused lift into the experiment, a dry drop on the verdict. Give “independent ways to move” a clear explanatory cadence. Three cost phrases land rhythmically; don't shout them. Finish the budget joke deadpan.

Pronunciations: Boston Dynamics = BOSS-tun dye-NAM-iks; Atlas = AT-lus; actuators = AK-choo-ay-turz. Say “thirteen,” not “one-three.” No synthetic accents, forced laughter, or narrator impression of a Boston Dynamics employee.

The same missing-digit visual answers what “this” means immediately. Use a finger-count gesture and a clearly outlined empty pinky position. Keep all later narration unchanged. Record only the necessary opening pickup if the tool supports a clean matched performance. Compare both openings internally; the default is A.
