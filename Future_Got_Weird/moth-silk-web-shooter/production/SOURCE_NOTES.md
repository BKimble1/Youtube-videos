## Depiction choices

- **Scale.** The lab is built in true metres: bench, 9 cm dish, 42 mm blade, 12 cm gap, 13 cm handheld nozzle. The
  fiber is drawn a few pixels wide (real ones range from a hair's width to about 0.5 mm) so that one strand stays
  visible at phone size.
- **Timing.** The fiber's 12 cm flight is shown slowed (about 2.6 s) so "forms in midair" can be seen; the street jet
  is in an explicit slow-motion (time visibly slows and returns).
- **Apparatus.** A two-syringe pump and a handheld coaxial nozzle: a plausible bench arrangement for the documented
  coaxial-needle method, not a copy of the lab's actual hardware (which could not be inspected).
- **The blade's motion.** Tension first (the fiber goes taut while the blade waits), then the exposed end rises
  about the buried tip, then the tip slides out along its own axis; from there a small rigid-body simulation (gravity,
  damping, the inextensible fiber, the tray floor) carries it, so it never moves before tension, never detaches and
  never teleports. Sand grains fall from the part that was buried.
- **Safety.** Gloves, glasses and a lab coat in the lab; her hands never cross the blade's edge or path; the blade ends
  in the tray, not near her face.

## The opening is imaginary

Nothing in the film presents the chase as real footage, news or something the scientists did. It must not be used as a
thumbnail claim that a fluid can catch criminals.
