They fired a laser into a thunderstorm—and the lightning followed it.

You see, air normally resists the flow of electricity.

But this machine fired a thousand laser pulses every second, stripping electrons from air molecules and heating a narrow channel through the sky.

That gave electricity an easier path to travel.

So when lightning erupted upward from a nearby tower, part of the bolt followed that channel for over 160 feet.

A normal lightning rod only reaches as high as its metal tip.

But these scientists extended one into the sky—

using the air itself.
