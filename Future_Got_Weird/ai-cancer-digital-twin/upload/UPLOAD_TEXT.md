## Description

This microscopic DNA device hides a pattern that can push a cancer cell to destroy itself — and it only opens in
acidic surroundings. Now imagine choosing an attack like that by testing it first on a digital model of your own
tumor.

That complete system is a possible future, not something that exists today. AI has not cured all cancer. This video
follows separate pieces researchers are building — from different teams, technologies and stages of evidence — and
asks how they might one day fit together:

• Digital twins: mathematical models of particular tumors, built from measurements such as MRI and updated as new
  data arrive. They answer bounded questions and carry uncertainty; a simulated success still needs real-world
  testing. [1–3]
• Antibody-drug conjugates: an antibody carries a drug payload to a surface target; the cell takes it inside, and
  in some designs enzymes cut the linker to release the drug. Some are approved treatments — and they can still
  cause serious side effects. [4–5]
• AI-designed targeting proteins tested as recognition parts in experimental (preclinical) CAR immune-cell
  therapies. [6]
• A DNA "robotic switch" from Karolinska Institutet that hides a peptide pattern and exposes it under acidic
  conditions, clustering death receptors and triggering cell death. Tested in cells and in mice with breast-cancer
  tumors, where it reduced tumor growth compared with an inactive version. Not a human treatment, and not presented
  as an AI invention. [7–8]
• AI tools that predict drug response and resistance from single-cell tumor data (research stage). [9]

The connections between these examples are an editorial "what if" scenario. Animations are simplified and
exaggerated in scale, colour and timing to make mechanisms visible; they are not structural models.
