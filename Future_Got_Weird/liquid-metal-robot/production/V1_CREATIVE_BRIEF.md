## Exact visual story

The timestamps below are the creator's initial targets. Reconcile them with the word timings above.

### Scene 1 (0.00–2.40): the impossible escape

- Start directly on the miniature metal figure inside the cage. Its feet are already turning liquid on frame zero.
- Within the first half-second, the melting becomes unmistakable. The upper body collapses progressively into shiny
  liquid metal.
- The puddle moves toward the bars, squeezes through the narrow gaps and collects outside.
- Use the original winning animation as the primary source.
- Requirements:
  - the figure is immediately recognisable;
  - the melting is obvious before the first second ends;
  - the cage must not obscure the most important action;
  - no establishing shot, no intro;
  - no camera movement that makes the escape hard to read.

### Scene 2 (2.40–6.00): the metal reforms

- Preserve the existing magnet-guided movement into a figure-shaped mould. The metal fills the shape and cools into a
  solid miniature figure.
- Clearly show that a mould and external magnetic control are involved. Do not imply the figure reconstructs itself.
- End with the figure solid again, outside the cage. This is the first major payoff.

### Scene 3 (≈6.00–10.00): zoom inside the metal

Narration: "It's made from gallium packed with tiny magnetic particles."
- Push smoothly into the newly solidified figure. The camera travels into the surface instead of cutting to an
  unrelated diagram.
- Reveal the interior through a sophisticated macro-scale cross-section. Tiny magnetic microparticles are suspended
  throughout the gallium matrix and must feel embedded in a real material, not like abstract coloured dots floating in
  space.
- Use an appealing metallic texture, realistic highlights and clear depth layering.
- Keep the camera moving while the structure becomes visible.
- This must be as satisfying as the internal anatomy reveals in Cyborg Cockroach V5.

### Scene 4 (≈10.00–14.50): how the shape change works

Narration: "A magnetic field heats it into liquid. Another magnet steers the metal. And when it cools, it hardens
again."
- Move seamlessly from the particle view back to the physical lab setup.
- Show an alternating magnetic field generated by an actual coil heating the metal. Use physical apparatus and
  temperature-dependent surface changes, not glowing radial rings. The solid softens into a liquid puddle.
- Show a separate external magnet moving relative to the puddle, making it flow in a controlled direction.
- Then show the metal cooling, firming up and becoming solid.
- The viewer should understand three things without labels:
  1. heating turns the metal liquid;
  2. magnetic forces steer it;
  3. cooling solidifies it.
- Keep the progression accurate and exceptionally clear. Don't spend seconds on particles drifting without a meaningful
  transformation.

### Scene 5 (≈14.50–17.00): the second shock

Narration: "But researchers tested something even stranger."
- As we pull back from the apparatus, reveal a transparent, physical, stomach-shaped experimental model on the same
  bench. It must read as a model, not a patient.
- A small spherical foreign object is visible inside it, and the metal device approaches it.
- Make it a smooth, continuous transition with the same metallic subject and visual identity. No floating anatomy
  diagram.
- The viewer should instantly understand that the same material is about to do a second, stranger task.

### Scene 6 (≈17.00–23.00): the stomach object retrieval

Narration: "Inside a model stomach, it melted around a trapped object, hardened, and carried it back out."

This is the second major visual payoff. Show it as one connected physical sequence:
1. The device moves toward the trapped sphere.
2. Controlled heating makes it soften and spread around the object.
3. The liquid metal encloses the object.
4. The metal cools and hardens into a gripping shape around the sphere.
5. External magnetic control pulls the now-solid assembly away with the object.

Guidance:
- The moment the metal wraps around the sphere should be one of the strongest visual moments in the whole Short.
- The viewer must clearly see the change from liquid to a solid grip.
- Use the transparent stomach model where needed to show the mechanics.

Accuracy:
- The published experiment used a model stomach and externally controlled magnetic material.
- No real human procedure, and no implication of approval for human use.
- No implication that the device thinks, chooses targets or operates autonomously.
- The demonstration is compressed for storytelling, but keep its genuine physical sequence.

### Scene 7 (≈23.00–27.00): the question and loop

Narration: "Would you let one inside you?"
- Show the device carrying the object out of the stomach model. Use a short, meaningful pull-back that reveals the
  device's tiny scale compared with the model. Keep the metal moving during the question.
- Then transition smoothly back toward the original figure and cage, so the final visual matches the beginning (a
  solid figure, the cage, the metal beginning to soften) and leads naturally into frame 0 when the Short loops.
- No subscribe request, logo, outro, end card or awkward silence.
- If a perfect loop would compromise the story or force an unnatural transition, prioritise the satisfying retrieval
  ending over a forced loop.

## Visual style rules

- 2D vector illustration with restrained 2.5D depth, parallax and layered compositing. No generic glossy CGI,
  game-cutscene rendering or inconsistent 3D objects.
- Preserve:
  - crisp silhouettes and consistent geometry;
  - smooth easing and controlled camera acceleration;
  - realistic liquid motion and metallic surface reflections;
  - accurate shape transformation;
  - physical continuity between scenes;
  - detailed microscopic mechanisms;
  - clear, intentional scene changes.
- The liquid metal must look fluid and physically plausible:
  - no stretched polygons, broken highlights, abrupt colour changes, disconnected fragments or blobs that appear
    without cause;
  - a droplet behaves like fluid, not a morphing cartoon character;
  - metal never passes through solid surfaces, only through genuine openings and gaps.

## Retention

- A meaningful visual progression about every second: each change reveals new information, shows a consequence or
  advances the action. Constant motion is not the same as random motion.
- Priorities, in order:
  1. first-frame stopping power;
  2. the first-two-second transformation;
  3. a satisfying escape;
  4. the internal mechanism reveal;
  5. the second major experiment;
  6. a strong ending and loop.
- Avoid:
  - visually unchanged shots while the narration continues;
  - camera whips;
  - teleports between unrelated environments;
  - artificial zooms that reveal nothing.
- Track the same physical object into the next scene whenever possible.
- No floating explanation bubbles, radial diagrams, AI labels or decorative graphics.

## Audio and captions

- Restrained effects that add impact without competing with the narration:
  - metallic softening and liquefaction;
  - droplets coalescing;
  - magnet and lab-equipment movement;
  - mechanical activation;
  - gentle metallic solidification;
  - a satisfying contact when the object is captured;
  - subtle stomach-model ambience.
- No repetitive synthetic whooshes, distracting music or intermittent loud hums.
- Use the existing FGW captions:
  - large, readable white type with a dark outline;
  - selected yellow emphasis words;
  - correctly synchronised, never far ahead of the words;
  - consistent size and position;
  - never covering the important transformation.

## Science

Reference: Qingyuan Wang et al., "Magnetoactive liquid-solid phase transitional matter," *Matter* (2023),
DOI 10.1016/j.matt.2022.12.003.
- The material is gallium with embedded NdFeB microparticles (melting point about 29.8 °C).
- An alternating magnetic field heats it by induction; magnetic actuation moves it and controls its shape; cooling
  returns it to solid.
- Reshaping the minifigure required a mould.
- Object retrieval was demonstrated in a model stomach: it melted slightly to wrap around a small ball, solidified, and
  was magnetically moved out.
- These were lab demonstrations, not clinical use.
- Do not combine demonstrations from other materials or studies.
