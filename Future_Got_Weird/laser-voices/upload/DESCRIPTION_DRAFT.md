**Title:** This Laser Makes You Hear Voices

**Description:**

A laser that puts a voice right beside your ear, and nobody next to you hears it.

In 2019, researchers at MIT Lincoln Laboratory used an infrared laser (about 1.9 µm, a wavelength water vapour
absorbs) to make sound in the air itself: the air near the listener absorbs the light, warms, expands and sends out tiny
pressure waves. Sweeping the beam at the speed of sound concentrates that sound at one distance, so a message reaches
one person rather than everyone the beam passes. They reported conversational-level sound at about 2.5 m (8 ft).

The animation is illustrated: the infrared beam is invisible, and the dashed path, molecules and pressure fronts are
simplified drawings. The "voice" is a stylised stand-in; coverage describes the swept method's sound as louder but
lower in fidelity than the modulated method.
