Purpose: S6 turns the researchers' argument (Kalai et al. 2025) into a game show the viewer can follow. Under 1/0/0 grading, a guesser who knows exactly what an honest player knows still wins 7–6. Change one rule (wrong = −1) and the honest player wins 6–4. The trophy walking back is the punchline. This is the episode's causal 'why', and it sets up S7's real evidence (9 of 10 benchmarks grade right/wrong with no 'I don't know' credit). The story, cast and jokes are right. The execution is not.

Biggest execution problems:
(1) DEAD TIME. 22.2 of 46.8 s (47.5%) has nothing changing on screen: 15 still runs, the longest 2.73 s. The host holds the same raised-right-hand pose for about 42 s (f4441–5704). The honest contestant doesn't change from f4783 to f5286 (16.8 s). The guesser's arms-up pose is frozen for 6.3 s. Scores change by instant value swaps. Nobody presses a buzzer, points at a card or touches anything.

Each podium therefore sits 150 px right of its contestant, with its lid at shoe height (y≈860) and its base cut off by the frame bottom. The lid hides each contestant's right shoe. Contestants stand beside low boxes, not behind podiums. Scoreboards float on the curtain at shoulder height. The two 10-tile strips float above heads with a 70 px gap, so they read as one 20-tile strip. The trophy walks through the host and ends on the floor, not on a podium.

(4) TIMING AND LOGIC BUGS. `at('s22','Six')` resolves to the first 'six' ('know six answers', f4838), so the honest '6' appears before his blanks exist and 'Six points.' gets nothing. The guesser's board jumps '–'→7 and never shows his 6. The lucky tile resolves first, in the same way as the wrong ones, and the celebration lands on 'Seven' instead of after the failures. The s24 headline shows 'Somehow, a trophy.' at 30% opacity before it is spoken. The guesser's equation appears during the honest line. (5) READABILITY AND OVERLAPS. Overlays live outside the Camera and collide with the world:
