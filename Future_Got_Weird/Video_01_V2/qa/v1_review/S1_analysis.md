# V1 shot review — S1 — The counter (cold open), narration s01–s05, V1 frames 0–820 (0:00.0–0:27.3)

S1 is the cold open. It asks one checkable question, shows three AI "clerks" confidently handing over three different answers, stamps all of them WRONG, reveals that the man in the question is the lead author of the paper that ran the test, and lands the first joke ("Very professional. Very fictional."). It has to carry the brief's opening escalation: these systems sound certain → they disagree → they're all wrong. V1 has the right content but the execution is mechanical.

Biggest execution problems:
(1) Too much of the scene is frozen. 38.8% of it (10.6 s of 27.3 s) is still, across 8 runs. The longest are 2.23 s on the paper header (f660–726), 1.83 s and 1.67 s during the question (f145–199, f33–82), and about 4 s more where only blinks move (f343–393, f411–442, f451–493).
(2) The answer stage is disconnected. Slips fade and drop in from nowhere instead of being handed over. The stamp arm is a 1,500 px horizontal bar that enters from off-screen left and cuts across the clerks' chests. The stamper lands about 240 px from where WRONG prints. The year rings miss the year on all three slips (slip A's ring circles "(completed" and strikes through "is entitled").
(3) The WRONG stamps are the climax but have no anticipation, impact, response or settle. They register as less motion than either camera move.
(4) Readability. "Different" is never shown, and slips B and C are only ever seen at 27 px (about 9 px on a phone). Factual guard-rail text is 15–24 px (5–8 px on a phone): the ticket label, the slip date lines, the paper's source tag and the "Lead author" tag.
(5) The joke ending stacks four changes in 0.8 s, shows the punchline at 30% opacity before it is spoken, exposes the left end of the counter, and wipes into an empty cream frame.

V2 code alert: the V2 text changes s03 to "Three different answers… None of them are right…", so at('s03','All') will throw. The stamp and 'polish' cues must be re-keyed (stamps to 'None'), and the new pause after "answers…" needs designed anticipation.
