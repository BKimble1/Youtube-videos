What the scene does for the story: S2 is the hinge between the cold open and the explanation. It asks the episode's question ("how can a wrong answer sound that sure?") over ChatGPT's stamped-WRONG slip and the title. It then previews the three claims the film unpacks (writes what's likely → S3/S4; checking is a separate job → S8/S9; tests reward guessing → S6/S7) and brands the channel in the pause before S3. Its first 3 s fall inside the brief's "first ~30 seconds" polish window.

The five biggest execution problems:
(1) It is the stillest scene in the film. 12.7 of 17.0 s are still (74.3%), and its mean motion energy of 0.393 is the lowest of the ten scenes. Every element arrives as a 12–16-frame opacity fade with a 24–30 px rise and then freezes. The panel-2 caption fade (f1123–1135) changes so little (mean diff 0.035, under the 0.05 threshold) that motion.json counts all of f1085–1173 (2.97 s) as still.
(2) The three mechanisms don't perform their claims, and two of them contradict themselves. Panel 1 highlights the 'ai' tile on 'likely', yet its output slip has read '…in 2002 at CMU' since before any choice was made. Panel 3 previews S6's 7–6 with the sides and colours swapped: in S2 the coral guesser is on the LEFT and an ink honest board on the RIGHT, while S6 has the teal honest board on the LEFT and the coral guesser on the RIGHT. The EVIDENCE CHECK shutter height is a hard-coded constant, and the scoreboards appear already at 7 and 6.
(3) Composition and blank space. f824–827 are 100% blank cream, and f952–975 are blank except the 314×54 chip. Panel 1 sits alone in the left 29% of the frame for 3.2 s (f976–1073). (4) Readability, crops and collisions. Panel-internal text is 16–26 px, which is 5–9 px on a phone; the 'guesser'/'honest' labels are 16 px.
