# Future Got Weird — Video 01 — V2 production pass (brief, as given)

Take the completed V1 and turn it into the first genuinely professional finished version of this episode.
Do not redesign the video. Do not significantly shorten it. Do not replace the visual identity that is already working.

V1 already has the right story, illustrations, color palette, characters, metaphors, humor, structure and an
approximately 4:40 runtime. The issue is execution. Right now there are moments where it still feels like
illustrations being moved around on a timeline. V2 should make it feel like the scenes were actually directed and
animated.

Before editing anything, watch V1 from beginning to end and inspect the existing project. Build a shot-by-shot
understanding of what the viewer is supposed to look at, what the narration is saying, and what each animation is
supposed to communicate. Then improve the entire film.

## The central V2 goal

Do not make the video feel faster by cutting it shorter. Make it feel faster because the visuals continue developing
while the narration continues. A shot should rarely reach its final visual state and then just sit there. Instead:
setup → action → reaction → secondary movement → next development. The viewer should continually have something
meaningful to follow.

## New narration

Replace the narration with the newly created ElevenLabs test voice ("Test Voice"). Generate it in logical sections so
individual lines can be directed. The voice should feel young, intelligent, conversational, curious, energetic and
lightly funny. Not announcer-like, overly deep, fake enthusiastic, podcast-radio polished, monotone AI, or an imitation
of any specific real person. The rhythm should change naturally. Important reveals have tiny pauses. Jokes have
timing. Explanations move confidently.

- "Three different answers." Small pause. "None of them are right." That second line should land.
- "A confident font is still just a font." Give that line room to breathe.
- "One lucky guess. Three wrong answers. Somehow, a trophy." The final phrase dry and slightly amused.
- "The unglamorous move: check." Simple and confident.
- The final SOUNDING RIGHT / BEING RIGHT section slows slightly and feels conclusive.

Retime the animation around the natural voice. Do not speed up or stretch the voice to preserve V1 frame timings.
Runtime may move slightly but stays approximately the same.

## The big animation change

V1 has too many moments that behave like: object appears → animation finishes → everything freezes → narration
continues. Fix that throughout. That does not mean everything should constantly move. Give scenes layers of movement:

- Primary movement: the thing actually being explained.
- Secondary movement: a character response, machine response, paper movement, UI change, drawer movement.
- Background life: very subtle blinking, posture shifts, moving belts, lights, parallax, paper settling, machine
  vibration, environmental movement.

At most a few things demand attention at once. Professional animation is movement that directs attention.

## Camera and composition

Problems visible in V1: important animation partially off-screen; the scorer disappearing during the token sequence;
text cropped; huge areas of unused blank space; characters awkwardly cut; props partially entering the screen for no
clear reason; objects no longer aligning after camera movement. Every camera move answers: what should the viewer be
looking at during this exact sentence? If a pan or zoom does not improve that, don't use it. Camera movement reveals
information or increases emphasis; it never exists merely because the scene needs motion.

## Specific shots

- **Answer stage.** Awkward geometry; the long red pointer/arm entering from the left looks accidentally cropped; cards,
  arms and characters feel disconnected. Recompose. When one answer becomes the focus, emphasise it and let the other two
  recede; make the pointer movement deliberate; subtle visible character reactions; the WRONG stamp an actual impact:
  anticipation → impact → tiny screen/object response → settle, with sound.
- **Token / conveyor.** Keep the metaphor; show the cycle: candidate tokens appear → probabilities change → one
  candidate becomes favored → the selected token physically separates → snaps/drops into the sentence → the sentence
  advances → the machine evaluates the next token. Understandable with the sound muted. When scorer and sentence
  relate, both stay visible; move toward the information, never away from it.
- **Library.** Fix the cabinet's open section that looks like an empty hole. Drawers behave like stylised drawers with
  depth. Character searches shelves → finds the index section → pulls the drawer → card slides out → character examines
  it → the card's information becomes the focal point. The card is part of the scene, not floating. Subtle parallax, eye
  movement, tiny book/shelf activity, drawer mechanics.
- **Documents / evidence.** Don't treat the whole page as equally important. Zoom into the title, the year, the table
  entry. "Here is the exact evidence proving that."
- **Game show.** Keep roughly its length. Each question: question → contestant → choice → result → scoreboard response.
  Wrong answers feel different from correct ones; the score updates mechanically; characters react. The lucky correct
  answer gets a deliberately excessive little celebration after the failures. Then 7–6, trophy, "One lucky guess. Three
  wrong answers. Somehow, a trophy." — one of the best comedy beats. Scoreboard visible whenever its numbers matter.
- **Evidence-checking machine.** CLAIM → does the cited source exist? → retrieve source → does it support the claim? →
  NO → CLAIM FAILS. The object physically travels through the process (scanners, paper movement, indicators,
  mechanical responses). Satisfying and extremely clear.

## Transitions

A handful of smarter transitions where one scene motivates the next (answer card becomes a document; document moves into
the library; token becomes part of another element; scoreboard transforms toward the benchmark table; research paper
feeds the checking machine; verification result returns us to the courtroom/counter). Not every transition.

## Sound design

Substantially richer; every physical action gets feedback: answer cards, paper, WRONG stamp, pointer movement,
conveyor, token selection, token locking, probability changes, machine mechanisms, drawer opening, card removal, score
changes, wrong-answer buzz, correct-answer hit, trophy, document reveal, scanner, CLAIM FAILS, gavel, final branding.
Not wall-to-wall whooshes. Different sonic textures per category. Very low ambience under narration in some scenes
(conveyor room, library, game-show room, verification machine hum).

## Music

Not one level throughout: hook slightly more tension/curiosity; explanation quieter; game show more playful; real
evidence pulls back; final takeaway begins building; end card clean resolution. Narration always dominant.

## Small details

Weird open drawers, floating cards, characters not touching held objects, disconnected hands, odd overlaps, uneven
alignment, objects entering halfway from screen edges, clipping, strange scale changes, inconsistent line weights,
unnatural movement, instant stops, missing follow-through, accidental blank areas, text too close to frame edges.
Repair surgically without changing the art style.

## Motion quality

Weight: anticipation, acceleration, deceleration, small overshoot, recoil, settle. A card overshoots slightly and
settles; a drawer slows before the end; a stamp compresses on impact; a scoreboard number rolls/snaps; a token drops
with a tiny bounce. Subtle.

## Mobile readability

Answer text, scores, evidence, key labels, conclusions and diagrams must survive phone viewing. Environmental details
(book titles) can stay small. No camera move may make critical text unreadable.

## Opening

Disproportionate polish on the first ~30 seconds. Keep the cold open; no channel intro before it. Improve card reveals,
reactions, timing, pointer, stamps, camera placement, sound and escalation between the three answers: these systems
sound certain → they disagree → they're all wrong.

## Ending

Keep the return to the counter ("courtroom"). SOUNDING RIGHT and BEING RIGHT extremely clean and visually dominant;
reduce anything competing. Let the final source/gavel joke land. Transition cleanly into FUTURE GOT WEIRD / AI moves
fast. We make it make sense. End card long enough for a YouTube end screen, without a dead-feeling hold.

## Do not over-correct

No hyperactive TikTok edit, no constant camera shake, don't animate every background object, don't cover scenes in
motion graphics, don't replace the illustration language, don't change good jokes. Controlled, confident animation.
Quiet moments should feel intentionally quiet, not unfinished.

## Final QC

Watch once with no audio; listen once with no video; watch at phone size; watch normally without stopping. Fix any
awkward crop, off-screen element, broken asset, dead animation, unnecessary camera move, strange silence around a
visual action, unnatural voice line or overly long static hold before rendering.

compressed preview. Preserve all V2 project changes as the new base.
